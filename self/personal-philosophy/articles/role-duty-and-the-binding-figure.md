# Role, Duty & the Binding Figure

`PP_ROLE` · [← Personal Philosophy](../index.md)

The structural position whose steadiness is what lets everyone else be less steady — the patriarch, the founder, the fixed point.

## Questions

- Is the binding figure a real structural necessity in groups, or a cultural arrangement other forms could replace?
- What does occupying it cost, and is the cost payable indefinitely?
- Can role-duties bind someone who never consented to the role?
- How does an ethics centered on the individual accommodate obligations that come from position?
- What separates the binding figure from the merely controlling one?

## Challenges & perspectives

- Role morality: duties attach to the position, not the person, and change what identical behavior means.
- Jungian/archetypal reading of the Great Father as order-giving and potentially tyrannical.
- Role conflict and role strain as the standard failure modes.
- Feminist critique: the patriarch role is a historical power arrangement, not a natural structure.
- Distributed-leadership alternative: groups can cohere without a single fixed point.
