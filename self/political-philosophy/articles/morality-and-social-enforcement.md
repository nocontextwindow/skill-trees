# Morality & Social Enforcement

`POL_MORALENF` · [← Political Philosophy](../index.md)

Whether society may enforce a moral code beyond preventing force and fraud.

## Questions

- May society enforce morality beyond preventing harm? Where exactly is my line, and am I consistent?
- Is informal enforcement — shame, ostracism — categorically different from legal coercion, or just cheaper?
- Is a shared moral code a real precondition of stability, or a claim made by whoever holds the current code?
- Who decides which morality gets enforced when a society is genuinely plural?
- What's the strongest case for a purely non-enforcing position?

## Challenges & perspectives

- Devlin: a shared morality is part of what holds a society together, so its erosion is a real harm.
- Hart's reply: disintegration is asserted, not shown, and the argument would license enforcing any majority view.
- Informal enforcement operates whether or not law does — and answers to nobody.
- Haidt's moral foundations: liberals and conservatives are not disagreeing on one axis but weighting different ones.
- Toleration paradox: an order tolerant of everything can't defend itself against the intolerant.
