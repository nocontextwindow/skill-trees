# Statics

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_STATICS`

Forces and moments in equilibrium — the foundational skill of structural
thinking, and the one that transfers furthest. The real content is not the
equations but the free-body diagram: deciding what to cut out, what to
represent as a force, and how to idealize a joint as pinned or fixed. Those
idealizations are choices, not facts, and a real connection is never
perfectly either. Determinate structures can be solved from equilibrium
alone; indeterminate ones — which is nearly everything actually built —
cannot, and require stiffness and compatibility, meaning force distribution
depends on how stiff each member is. Load path intuition, the ability to
trace how a force reaches the ground, is the thing experienced engineers
have and beginners don't.

## Open questions

- How much error does idealizing a connection as pinned or fixed actually
  introduce, and when does it matter?
- What does it take to develop real load-path intuition rather than
  equation-following, and can that be practiced deliberately?
- Why does indeterminacy make a structure both safer and harder to analyze,
  and how should that tradeoff be weighed?
- Where does rigid-body statics stop being an adequate model of a real
  structure?
- Is stability a separate concept from equilibrium or a special case of it,
  and does treating them separately help?
- What is the smallest set of hand checks that would catch a badly wrong
  computer model?
