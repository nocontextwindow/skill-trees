# Construction Management

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_CONST`

Scheduling, cost, procurement, contracts, and site delivery — where a
design becomes a built object, and where most project failures actually
happen. The technical content (critical path method, earned value,
estimating) is straightforward; the hard part is that the contract
structure determines who carries which risk, and that allocation drives
behaviour more than any schedule does. Design-bid-build, design-build, and
integrated delivery are different answers to the same question about where
risk should sit. Large infrastructure projects have a well-documented and
persistent record of cost and schedule overrun, attributed variously to
optimism bias, strategic misrepresentation during bidding, and genuine
scope uncertainty — and which explanation you accept determines what
remedy makes sense.

## Open questions

- Are megaproject overruns caused by bad estimating or by incentives that
  reward optimistic bids — and what would distinguish those empirically?
- Which delivery method suits which project, and is that judgment or
  ideology in current practice?
- Where should risk sit to be priced honestly rather than merely
  transferred to whoever has least ability to refuse it?
- How much does the technical scheduling apparatus actually control an
  outcome versus document it after the fact?
- What is the honest tradeoff between designing thoroughly upfront and
  starting early with unresolved scope?
- How does the engineer's professional obligation interact with a client's
  commercial pressure when they conflict on site?
