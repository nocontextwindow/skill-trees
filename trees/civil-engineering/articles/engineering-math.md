# Engineering Math

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_MATH`

Calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, and numerical methods as
used rather than as proved. Engineering math is a distinct dialect: the
emphasis is on setting up the right equations, knowing which
approximations are safe, and having a feel for magnitudes, not on rigour.
The permanent question is how much of the underlying mathematics is
load-bearing now that solvers do the computation — the case for
understanding it is not that you'll do it by hand but that you need to
recognize when the solver is producing garbage, which requires knowing what
conditioning, convergence, and discretization error actually are.
Dimensional analysis and order-of-magnitude estimation are the highest
return-on-effort tools in the whole subject and the ones most often skipped.

## Open questions

- Which parts of this are worth deep understanding versus competent tool
  use, given that software does the arithmetic?
- What does it take to recognize a numerically wrong answer that looks
  plausible?
- How much intuition can dimensional analysis really provide before the
  detailed model, and where does it mislead?
- Where do linearization and superposition quietly stop being valid in real
  structural and hydraulic problems?
- Is there value in hand-calculation practice beyond building intuition, or
  is that a nostalgic defence of a dead skill?
- How do I keep a sense of magnitude when every intermediate quantity comes
  out of a black box?
