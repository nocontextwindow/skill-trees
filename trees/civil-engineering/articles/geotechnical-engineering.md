# Geotechnical Engineering

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_GEOTECH`

Soil and rock behaviour, foundations, retaining structures, and slope
stability — the branch where the material is given rather than specified,
and cannot be inspected in full. Everything else in civil engineering works
with materials of known, tested, manufactured properties; geotechnical
engineering works with whatever is under the site, characterized by a
handful of boreholes interpolating across a volume that may be wildly
heterogeneous. Effective stress and the role of pore water pressure are the
central idea and the source of most catastrophic failures, since soil
strength depends on drainage conditions and rate of loading. Consequently
this is the sub-discipline with the largest uncertainties, the biggest
gap between theory and observed behaviour, and the strongest tradition of
the observational method — design, monitor, and adapt during construction.

## Open questions

- How much can a handful of boreholes really tell me about a site, and how
  should that uncertainty propagate into the design?
- When is the observational method responsible engineering and when is it a
  way of starting construction before the design is understood?
- Why are geotechnical safety factors so much larger than structural ones —
  is that calibrated or historical?
- How should drained versus undrained behaviour change what I consider the
  governing case, and how often is that judgment call gotten wrong?
- What do the large historical failures have in common, and is it a
  knowledge gap or an investigation-scope gap?
- Where do constitutive soil models add predictive power over simple
  empirical correlations, and where do they just add parameters?
