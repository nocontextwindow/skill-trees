# Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_FLUID`

Flow in pipes, channels, and porous media, plus the hydrology that
supplies the design flows. Fluid mechanics is where closed-form solutions
mostly stop: turbulence has no general analytical treatment, so practice
runs on empirical correlations (Manning's n, friction factors, loss
coefficients) whose coefficients are calibrated rather than derived, and
whose precision is far lower than the number of digits usually reported.
Dimensional analysis and similitude do enormous work here, since physical
and computational modelling both depend on matching the right dimensionless
groups. The other hard part is hydrology: a "100-year flood" is a
probabilistic statement estimated from short and increasingly
non-stationary records, and treating it as a fixed design condition is a
known and unresolved problem.

## Open questions

- What is a design flood recurrence interval actually worth when the record
  is short and the climate is non-stationary?
- How accurate are the standard empirical coefficients, and how much of
  hydraulic design precision is illusory?
- When does CFD earn its cost over a correlation or a physical model, and
  how would I validate it?
- Where does the assumption of steady, uniform flow break down badly enough
  to change a design decision?
- How should uncertainty in an extreme-event estimate be communicated to
  someone making a funding decision?
- Which dimensionless groups actually have to match for a scale model to
  mean anything, and what do you give up when they can't all match?
