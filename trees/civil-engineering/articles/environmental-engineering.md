# Environmental Engineering

[← Civil Engineering](../index.md) · `CE_ENV`

Water and wastewater treatment, air quality, contaminated site
remediation, and solid waste — engineering where the design constraint is
usually a regulatory number rather than a physical limit. That is the
defining feature: the target concentration comes from a risk assessment and
a political process, and the treatment train is designed backwards from it,
which means understanding where the standard came from is as much a part of
the work as the process design. Treatment processes are largely biological
and therefore variable, slow to respond, and sensitive to upsets in ways
purely physical processes aren't. Life-cycle thinking regularly reverses
intuitive conclusions — the treatment with the cleanest effluent may have
the worst overall footprint once energy and residuals are counted.

## Open questions

- Where does a regulatory limit reflect a real health threshold and where
  is it a detection limit or a negotiated number?
- How should risk to a small number of people be weighed against cost borne
  by many, and who legitimately makes that call?
- When does life-cycle analysis genuinely change the right answer, and when
  is it used to justify a conclusion already reached?
- What do we actually do about contaminants that are regulated faster than
  they can be characterized?
- Is centralized or distributed infrastructure the better default for water
  and waste, and what does the answer depend on?
- How much does the biological variability of treatment processes limit how
  tightly effluent quality can be guaranteed?
