## Structural Analysis & Design (`CE_STRUCT`)

Structural analysis solves for internal forces and displacements in
statically indeterminate structures (more supports/members than
equilibrium alone can resolve) using methods like moment distribution, the
stiffness method, and — for anything nontrivial in practice — finite
element software. Structural design then takes those internal forces and
sizes members against a governing code: steel design follows AISC's LRFD
specification, reinforced concrete follows ACI 318, and both encode decades
of empirical safety margin on top of the underlying mechanics. The
persistent tension in the field is between analysis rigor and constructability
— the "correct" design on paper still has to be buildable, inspectable, and
tolerant of real-world construction error.

## Geotechnical Engineering (`CE_GEOTECH`)

Soil, unlike steel or concrete, is a naturally variable, particulate
material whose behavior depends heavily on moisture content, density, and
loading history, which is why geotechnical engineering leans so much on
site-specific testing (borings, standard penetration tests, triaxial
testing) rather than published material properties. Core topics are soil
classification, effective stress, consolidation and settlement, slope
stability, and foundation design (shallow vs. deep/pile foundations). It's
widely considered the civil discipline with the most irreducible
uncertainty, since you're characterizing a material you can't fully see
without disturbing it.
