## Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics (`CE_FLUID`)

Fluid mechanics establishes the governing relationships (continuity,
Bernoulli's equation, momentum conservation) for fluids in motion, and
hydraulics applies them to the civil-scale problems of moving water: pipe
networks, pumps, open-channel flow in rivers and canals, and stormwater
systems. Open-channel flow is the trickier half in practice because the
free surface means the flow depth is itself an unknown that has to be
solved for (normal depth, critical depth, hydraulic jumps), unlike pressurized
pipe flow where geometry is fixed. This section is the direct prerequisite
for both environmental engineering and a lot of real infrastructure design
work — culverts, dams, stormwater detention.

## Environmental Engineering (`CE_ENV`)

Environmental engineering applies fluid mechanics, chemistry, and
microbiology to the design of systems that protect public health and the
environment: drinking water treatment, wastewater treatment, air quality
control, and solid/hazardous waste management. Wastewater treatment in
particular is a good illustration of engineering built on biology — the
activated sludge process is literally cultivating a microbial community to
do the treatment work, and plant performance depends on keeping that
ecosystem healthy. The field operates under a dense regulatory layer (Clean
Water Act, Safe Drinking Water Act in the US, and analogues elsewhere)
that shapes design as much as the underlying science does.
