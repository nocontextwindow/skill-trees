## Transportation Engineering (`CE_TRANSPORT`)

Transportation engineering covers the planning, geometric design, and
operational analysis of roads, transit, and other networks: pavement
design (which draws on both materials science and the geotechnical
subgrade conditions below it), traffic flow theory (how vehicle density,
speed, and flow rate relate — including why a highway can suddenly jam with
no obvious cause, a phenomenon well modeled by traffic-flow shockwave
theory), and geometric design of curves, sight distance, and intersections.
It's also one of the more directly policy-entangled subfields, since
induced demand and land-use effects mean that adding capacity doesn't
always reduce congestion the way a naive model would predict.

## Construction Management (`CE_CONST`)

Construction management is where a completed design actually becomes a
physical structure, which means scheduling (critical path method network
diagrams), cost estimating, procurement, and contract structures (design-
bid-build vs. design-build vs. construction manager at risk) become the
central skills rather than mechanics or hydraulics. A design that's
technically optimal but ignores constructability, sequencing, or site
logistics routinely costs more and takes longer than a good-enough design
executed well, which is the main reason this sits as its own discipline
rather than an afterthought to design.
