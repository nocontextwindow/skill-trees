# Real Analysis

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_REALANAL`

Calculus rebuilt on foundations, and the place where the strangeness of the
continuum stops being avoidable: continuous functions that are nowhere
differentiable, uncountable sets of measure zero, and repeated failures of
the intuition that limits and other operations can be freely interchanged.
The construction of the reals is the crux of it. The continuum is a
specific and unforced choice — constructive and non-standard alternatives
are consistent and give different answers — which makes the usual
presentation, where the reals arrive as though they were the only option,
quietly misleading.

## Open questions

- Is the real line discovered or constructed? What is the status of the
  uncountably many reals no one will ever specify?
- Do pathological functions reveal something about the continuum, or
  something about the definitions we happened to choose?
- What is actually lost by working constructively — real mathematics, or
  only convenience?
- Rigor here mostly confirms what intuition already said. Why should I
  trust intuition in the cases nobody has checked?
- What was genuinely wrong with infinitesimals, given that non-standard
  analysis makes them work?
