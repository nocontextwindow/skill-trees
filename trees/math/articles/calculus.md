# Calculus

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_CALC`

Two ideas — the limit, and the theorem linking differentiation to
integration — buried under a large amount of technique. A genuine
historical fact worth sitting with: calculus was used successfully for
roughly two centuries before anyone could say what a limit was. Rigor
arrived long after the results, which raises a question about what rigor is
actually for. The multivariable extension is where geometric intuition
stops carrying you and where the real content becomes visible: the
derivative is a linear approximation, and that's the whole subject in one
sentence.

## Open questions

- If calculus worked for two centuries without foundations, what was doing
  the work? What does that say about rigor's role?
- Is the derivative best understood as a rate, a slope, or a linear
  approximation? Does the choice change which problems become visible?
- What is an infinitesimal? Was the epsilon-delta reconstruction the only
  way out, or just the one that won?
- How much of calculus is technique a machine should do, and what is left
  once that's subtracted?
- Why does the fundamental theorem hold? Is there an explanation, or only
  a proof?
