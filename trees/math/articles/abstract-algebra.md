# Abstract Algebra

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_ABSTRACT`

The move to studying structures by their axioms rather than by their
instances: groups, rings, fields. The payoff is that one theorem covers
every system with the given structure, and that structure explains results
which had looked like accidents — why there's no general formula for the
quintic, why certain constructions are impossible in principle rather than
merely hard. The cost, and the reason it's difficult, is that motivation
disappears: the definitions look arbitrary until you've met enough
instances to see what they were abstracted from.

## Open questions

- Are these structures discovered in the instances or imposed on them?
  What would distinguish the two?
- Why are the group axioms the right axioms? Is there a reason, or is it
  selection after the fact for whatever proved fruitful?
- Symmetry "explains" solvability. Is that an explanation, or a translation
  of the problem into a language where the answer is already visible?
- When abstraction strips away all motivation, is the abstraction premature
  or is the discomfort simply the price of admission?
- How much of mathematics is the study of what's forced, and how much of
  what happened to be interesting to someone?
