# Topology

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_TOPOLOGY`

Continuity abstracted away from distance: what's left of a space when only
nearness is kept. The definition by open sets looks unmotivated until you
see that it is exactly what continuity requires and nothing more. The
subject then splits in two. Point-set topology is a language most of modern
mathematics is written in; algebraic topology is a machine for turning
shape into algebra so that questions about holes become computations. Both
raise the same background worry — whether an abstraction is capturing
something real or generalizing until the content has drained out.

## Open questions

- Is the open-set definition the right abstraction of continuity, or the
  one that turned out to be workable?
- What is a hole, such that algebra can count them? Is the invariant
  capturing the shape or defining it?
- Where does topology stop being useful — when does discarding distance
  discard the thing I cared about?
- Is the point-set machinery content, or bookkeeping that lets the content
  be stated?
- Why should the shape of a space determine so much about the analysis and
  the physics that live on it?
