# Algebra & Precalculus

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_ALGEBRA`

The manipulative layer everything above it silently assumes. Its real
content is small: the idea of a function, and the discovery that symbolic
manipulation is a reliable substitute for reasoning about the quantities
themselves — you can push symbols correctly without holding the meaning in
mind. That's the source of algebra's power and of most people's
estrangement from it at the same time. Nearly everything later diagnosed as
conceptual difficulty in calculus or analysis turns out on inspection to be
a fluency gap here.

## Open questions

- What is actually acquired when symbolic manipulation becomes automatic,
  and what is lost while it stays effortful?
- Is a function a rule, a set of ordered pairs, or a process? Do those ever
  come apart in a way that matters?
- Which parts of this deserve memorization, and which deserve
  derivation-on-demand? What's the cost of getting that split wrong?
- Where are my own gaps, and is there any way to find them other than by
  hitting them years later?
- Why is this layer taught as procedure when everything built on it is
  taught as structure?
