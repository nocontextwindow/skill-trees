# Linear Algebra

[← Mathematics](../index.md) · `MATH_LINALG`

Probably the most used mathematics in existence, largely because linear
problems are the ones that can be solved, and everything else gets
linearized until it becomes one. The subject has two faces — matrix
computation and the coordinate-free theory of vector spaces and linear
maps — and they teach genuinely different things, which is why people
fluent in one can be lost in the other. Eigenvalues are where it stops
being bookkeeping: they say that a linear map is, in the right basis,
nothing but scaling, which is why they surface in differential equations,
quantum mechanics, and network ranking alike.

## Open questions

- Is the dominance of linear methods a fact about the world, or about which
  problems we're able to solve?
- What is a vector, once I refuse to answer "a list of numbers"?
- Why do eigenvalues carry so much information — what is it about scaling
  directions that encodes so much of a map's behavior?
- Coordinates or no coordinates: which view should be primary, and what
  does each one hide?
- What breaks first when a system is genuinely nonlinear, and how would I
  know I've passed that point rather than assume I haven't?
