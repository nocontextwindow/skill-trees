## Corporate Finance (`FIN_CORP`)

The finance function from inside a company rather than as an outside
investor: capital structure (how much debt vs. equity to fund the business
with), the cost of capital (WACC), dividend and buyback policy, and
capital budgeting (which projects to actually invest in, using NPV/IRR).
The Modigliani-Miller theorem is the load-bearing theoretical result: in a
frictionless world with no taxes or bankruptcy costs, capital structure
shouldn't affect firm value at all — which is a deliberately unrealistic
baseline whose real value is in showing exactly which frictions (the tax
shield on debt interest, bankruptcy risk, agency costs between managers and
shareholders) actually explain why real capital-structure choices matter as
much as they do in practice.

## Venture Capital & Network Power (`FIN_VC`)

How startup financing actually works — staged rounds, preferred stock and
liquidation preferences, the power-law return distribution VC funds
depend on (a handful of outlier investments, not a diversified average,
drive fund returns), and why that power law shapes everything from how
VCs pick founders to how aggressively they compete for access to the best
deals. Beyond the mechanics, VC firms like a16z operate as much on network
and reputation power as on capital itself: being the fund a hot founder
chooses to take money from (rather than the reverse) is a form of status
and access economics that overlaps directly with the frame-control and
modern-institutional-power material elsewhere in this repo. Marc
Andreessen's "It's Time to Build" essay and a16z's American Dynamism thesis
are useful primary sources for how the industry's most influential
practitioners narrate their own role.

## Cryptocurrency & Digital Assets (`FIN_CRYPTO`)

Blockchain-based money and assets, starting from Bitcoin's core innovation
(a decentralized, trust-minimized ledger secured by proof-of-work, solving
the double-spend problem without a central authority) through Ethereum's
generalization into programmable smart contracts, and the resulting
ecosystem of DeFi, stablecoins, and NFTs. The field is genuinely split
between a "digital gold" / sound-money case (a fixed, algorithmically
enforced supply as a hedge against monetary debasement — echoing the
hard-money critique from the Economics tree) and a skeptical case that most
crypto assets are speculative vehicles without underlying cash flows or
intrinsic value, closer to collectibles than investments. Whatever one's
view on the assets, the underlying technology (distributed consensus,
cryptographic verification of state) has proven genuinely useful outside
speculation, which is a separate question from whether any given token is
well-valued.
