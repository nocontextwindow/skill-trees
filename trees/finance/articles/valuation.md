# Valuation

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_VALUE`

Estimating what an asset is worth, as distinct from what it currently
costs. Two families dominate: discounted cash flow, which is internally
consistent and outsources everything to your own forecasts, and
comparables, which outsources judgment to whatever the market is
currently paying for similar things. Both produce a number with far more
apparent precision than the inputs support, and in most DCFs the terminal
value — the least defensible part — is the majority of the answer.
Valuation is best treated as a structured way to state assumptions rather
than as a measurement.

## Open questions

- Terminal value is usually most of a DCF — is the exercise measuring
  anything the analyst didn't put in?
- Comparables outsource judgment to the market; DCF outsources it to your
  own forecasts. Which failure mode is more recoverable?
- What's the honest output of a valuation — a point, a range, or a set of
  scenarios with their assumptions attached?
- When does the market price contain information my model lacks, and how
  do I distinguish that from being right and early?
- How should optionality be valued without turning every unfavorable
  forecast into "optionality"?
- Does valuing an asset make sense at all where the exit depends entirely
  on a future buyer's appetite rather than on cash flows?
