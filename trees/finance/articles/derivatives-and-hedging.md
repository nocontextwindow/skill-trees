# Derivatives & Hedging

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_DERIV`

Contracts whose value depends on something else, priced by the argument
that a payoff which can be replicated must cost what replicating it
costs. That no-arbitrage logic is the most elegant reasoning in finance
and rests on assumptions — continuous trading, no gaps, unlimited
liquidity, a counterparty that pays — which fail together and precisely
when the payoff matters. The volatility surface is the market's standing
admission that the canonical model is wrong, and the industry keeps
quoting in that model's units anyway, which is worth understanding rather
than dismissing.

## Open questions

- Replication assumes continuous trading and no gaps — what does the
  price mean when both fail at once?
- The volatility surface concedes the model is wrong; why does quoting in
  a wrong model's units persist, and what does it actually communicate?
- Where is the line between hedging and taking a position? Does the
  accounting treatment settle that or obscure it?
- Do derivatives distribute risk to those best able to bear it, or
  concentrate it where it's least visible?
- What does counterparty risk do to a "risk-free" replication argument?
- Is a hedge that requires being right about timing a hedge at all?
