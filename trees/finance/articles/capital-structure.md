# Capital Structure & Financing

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_CAPSTRUCT`

How a firm funds itself, and whether the mix matters. The
Modigliani-Miller result is usually taught as "it doesn't" and is more
useful read backwards: since structure clearly does matter in the world,
the result tells you the effect must come from taxes, distress costs,
information asymmetry, or incentives — and names the list. Competing
accounts (trade-off theory, pecking order) each explain part of observed
behavior and neither explains the strong industry clustering of leverage
ratios, which suggests something the firm-level theories don't capture.

## Open questions

- MM says structure is irrelevant absent frictions — so which friction is
  doing the work in any specific real decision?
- Is there an optimal leverage ratio, or only a range wide enough that
  nothing forces a change?
- Does debt discipline management, or just make the firm fragile in a way
  that resembles discipline until it doesn't?
- Why do capital structures cluster by industry more than by the
  firm-level factors theory says should drive them?
- What is the real cost of financial distress short of bankruptcy, and
  how would anyone measure it?
- Does the choice of financing signal something to outsiders that the
  firm can't credibly say directly?
