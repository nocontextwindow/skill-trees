# Quantitative Foundations

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_MATH`

The probability and statistics that finance runs on: returns and their
distributions, volatility, correlation, and the estimation of all three
from a short and non-stationary history. The mathematics itself is not
difficult relative to other applied fields; the difficulty is that the
data violates the assumptions in specific, known ways — fat tails,
volatility clustering, correlations that shift precisely in the periods
you were trying to protect against. Knowing which assumption is
load-bearing in a given result is most of the skill.

## Open questions

- Which standard results depend on normality, and how badly does each
  degrade under fat tails?
- Is volatility a property of an asset, or an artifact of the frequency
  at which it's sampled?
- What does an estimated correlation mean when the relationship changes
  in exactly the regime you care about?
- How much financial mathematics is descriptive, and how much is a shared
  convention markets then quote in?
- When is an assumption a simplification, and when is it the entire
  source of the answer?
- With one non-repeatable history, how much can be inferred about
  distributions of rare events at all?
