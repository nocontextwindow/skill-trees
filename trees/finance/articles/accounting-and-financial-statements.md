# Accounting & Financial Statements

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_ACCT`

The language firms use to describe themselves, and the raw material for
almost every valuation. Its structure — the three statements tying
together, accrual matching revenue to the effort that produced it — is
learnable in a few weeks. What takes longer is developing a sense for
where management discretion lives: revenue recognition timing,
capitalization choices, reserve levels, what gets adjusted out of a
non-GAAP figure. Accounting is not a measurement of economic reality but
a set of conventions for approximating it, and the gaps are where the
interesting reading happens.

## Open questions

- Where is management discretion largest, and which line items would I
  check first for it?
- Accruals measure effort but can be steered; cash flow is harder to fake
  but doesn't measure value creation. Which do I anchor on, and when?
- What real economics do goodwill, capitalized costs, and off-balance
  sheet arrangements obscure?
- Do non-GAAP adjustments ever add information, or are they always
  advocacy?
- How much of a modern firm's economics is invisible in the statements
  entirely — intangibles, brand, accumulated human capital?
- Is conservatism in accounting standards a virtue, or a systematic bias
  that just happens to be in a comfortable direction?
