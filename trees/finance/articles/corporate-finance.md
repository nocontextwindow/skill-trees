# Corporate Finance

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_CORP`

The firm's own financial decisions: which projects to fund, how to
measure them, what to do with cash that has no better internal use.
Theory says take every positive-NPV project; practice uses payback
periods, IRR rankings, and hurdle rates well above cost of capital, which
either reflects unmodeled constraints (capital rationing, managerial
bandwidth, forecast unreliability) or is simply error. Underneath it all
sits the agency problem — the people allocating capital are not the
people whose capital it is — and most of the field's institutional
apparatus is an attempt to manage that.

## Open questions

- If NPV is right, why do firms use rules that overrule it — is that
  error, or an unmodeled constraint the theory should include?
- Which capital allocation decisions are actually made on analysis, and
  which are internal politics dressed as analysis?
- Do buybacks return capital efficiently or signal an absence of ideas?
  Can an outsider distinguish the two?
- What does an agency problem cost, and can any incentive scheme address
  it without creating a new one?
- Should a firm maximize shareholder value? If not, what objective is
  both different and actually operable?
- Is a conglomerate's internal capital market ever better than the
  external one, or is the discount always deserved?
