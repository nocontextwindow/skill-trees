# Time Value of Money

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_TVM`

Discounting is the single operation the rest of finance is built on: a
claim on future cash is worth less than cash now, and everything from
bond pricing to project selection is an application of that. The
arithmetic is trivial and the judgment is not, because the discount rate
compresses opportunity cost, risk, and time preference into one number
that then drives the entire answer. The term structure of rates is the
market's version of the same problem, and even there it is contested how
much of the curve is expectation and how much is compensation for bearing
duration.

## Open questions

- The discount rate does nearly all the work in a valuation — what
  justifies a particular number rather than a convention?
- Is discounting a statement about time preference, opportunity cost, or
  risk? Does conflating them cause real errors?
- What does the yield curve encode — expectations, term premia, or supply
  and demand for duration? Can those be separated in practice?
- How should very long-horizon cash flows be discounted, when a constant
  rate makes them arbitrarily small?
- Where does compounding intuition break down — sequence of returns, path
  dependence, arithmetic versus geometric means?
- Should the rate used to evaluate a decision differ from the rate the
  market would use, and on what grounds?
