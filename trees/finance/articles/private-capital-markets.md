# Private Capital Markets

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_PRIVATE`

Buyouts, private credit, and M&A — an increasing share of where capital
actually goes, and the part of finance with the weakest measurement.
Returns are reported as IRRs, which are sensitive to timing and to the
use of subscription lines, and marks are model-based and smooth, which
makes reported volatility low for reasons unrelated to underlying risk.
The decomposition question is the honest one: how much of the return came
from operational improvement, how much from leverage, how much from
buying and selling at different multiples, and how much from the interest
rate environment of the period.

## Open questions

- Is the illiquidity premium real compensation, or partly a fee paid for
  not being shown a mark-to-market price?
- IRR is manipulable by timing and leverage — what measure would let you
  honestly compare a fund against an index?
- Where does buyout return actually come from: operations, leverage,
  multiple expansion, or entry timing? What decomposition is defensible?
- If most acquisitions destroy value on average, why does the activity
  persist at scale?
- As more of the economy stays private, what does the public market's
  price signal still tell you about the whole?
- Do the governance advantages claimed for private ownership survive once
  the fund's own holding period is taken into account?
