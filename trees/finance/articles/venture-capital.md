# Venture Capital

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_VC`

Financing companies whose outcomes are extremely skewed — a small number
of investments produce nearly all returns, and most produce none. That
distribution shape drives everything about how the asset class operates:
portfolio sizing, staged financing with information revealed between
rounds, and a tolerance for total loss that would be irrational
elsewhere. It also makes attribution nearly impossible, since with
power-law outcomes and a handful of exits per fund, separating skill from
access from vintage-year luck requires more observations than a career
provides.

## Open questions

- With power-law outcomes, how many observations does it take to
  distinguish skill from having invested in a favorable period?
- Does an investor's involvement cause the outcome or select for it —
  what evidence would separate those?
- Staged financing solves an information problem and creates a hold-up
  problem. Where does that balance actually land?
- What does a valuation mean for a company with a preference stack, no
  market, and a price set by the marginal buyer of a small stake?
- How much of the asset class's historical return is attributable to the
  rate environment it grew up in?
- Does the structure of the fund — fee, carry, fixed life — distort the
  decisions in a direction predictable enough to price?
