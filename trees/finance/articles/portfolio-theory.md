# Portfolio Theory

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_PORT`

How holdings combine, which turns out to matter more than which holdings
they are. Diversification is the one effect in finance that reliably
improves the risk/return tradeoff without requiring a forecast. The
formal apparatus around it — mean-variance optimization, the efficient
frontier — is notoriously sensitive to estimated inputs, to the point
that naive equal weighting often outperforms an optimizer fed real
estimates. And diversification is measured by correlations that tend to
converge toward one in exactly the episodes it was supposed to protect
against.

## Open questions

- Mean-variance optimization is extremely sensitive to its inputs — is
  the framework useful, or is the useful part just "diversify"?
- Correlations converge in a crisis. Is there such a thing as robust
  diversification, or only diversification that hasn't been tested yet?
- How much should a portfolio be shaped by the liability or goal it funds
  versus by the efficient frontier?
- Is rebalancing risk control, a bet on mean reversion, or both — and
  what does it cost when the trend was real?
- What is the right amount of leverage, and why do the arguments for and
  against so rarely engage each other?
- Is concentration ever rational, and is the argument for it about
  information advantage or about tolerance for ruin?
