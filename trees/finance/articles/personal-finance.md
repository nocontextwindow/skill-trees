# Personal Finance

[← Finance](../index.md) · `FIN_PERSONAL`

The household as the applied case, where all the theory meets a single
non-diversifiable life. The arithmetic is unglamorous and mostly settled:
early on, the savings rate dominates returns, and taxes and fees are the
only certain returns available. What isn't settled is everything
structural — how a mortgage, a concentrated illiquid leveraged asset,
fits alongside a portfolio; how human capital should be treated as a bond
or an equity; how much risk a given horizon warrants. Underneath sits the
question people skip: what the money is actually for, without which most
portfolio questions have no answer.

## Open questions

- The savings rate dominates returns early and inverts later — where is
  the crossover for my actual situation?
- How much risk does a long horizon justify, and does the "equities are
  safer over long horizons" argument survive scrutiny?
- How should a mortgage — leverage on a concentrated illiquid asset — be
  treated alongside a portfolio?
- Taxes and fees are the only guaranteed returns; how far should a
  decision be distorted to capture them?
- Should human capital be treated as a bond-like or equity-like asset,
  and what does that imply for what's held around it?
- What is the money for? Most allocation questions can't be answered
  without that, and it usually goes unstated.
