## Chemistry Foundations (`BIO_CHEM`)

Biology is chemistry that has learned to copy itself, so the groundwork is
ordinary general and organic chemistry: covalent and hydrogen bonding,
functional groups, acid-base behavior, and reaction thermodynamics, plus
enough physical chemistry to make sense of free energy, entropy, and
equilibrium. The payoff is being able to read a biochemical pathway diagram
and actually understand why it runs downhill energetically, why enzymes
lower activation energy rather than change the equilibrium, and why water's
polarity is doing more structural work in a cell than any single molecule.
Skipping this and jumping straight to "biology" tends to produce people who
can name pathways but can't reason about why any step happens.

## Cell Biology (`BIO_CELL`)

The cell is the smallest unit that is unambiguously alive, and cell biology
is the study of how membranes, organelles, and molecular machines cooperate
to keep that unit self-maintaining and self-replicating. Central themes:
compartmentalization (why eukaryotes walled off the nucleus, mitochondria,
and ER, and what that buys them), the cell cycle and its checkpoints, and
signal transduction — how a cell senses and responds to its environment
without a nervous system. The endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and
chloroplasts is one of the best-evidenced deep-time events in biology and
is worth understanding not just as trivia but as the reason eukaryotic
cells can afford to be as large and complex as they are.
