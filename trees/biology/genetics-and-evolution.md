## Genetics & Molecular Biology (`BIO_GENETICS`)

The central dogma — DNA to RNA to protein — is the skeleton, but the
interesting material is everything that regulates and complicates it:
promoters and enhancers, epigenetic marks like methylation and histone
modification, alternative splicing, and the fact that most of the genome
doesn't code for protein at all. Classical (Mendelian) genetics gives you
the population-level logic of inheritance; molecular genetics gives you the
mechanism. CRISPR-Cas9 turned "editing a specific sequence in a living
genome" from a multi-year project into a routine one, which is why gene
editing ethics debates (germline editing, gene drives) have become urgent
rather than academic.

## Evolutionary Biology (`BIO_EVO`)

Evolution by natural selection requires just three ingredients — variation,
heritability, differential reproduction — but the mechanisms that produce
those ingredients (mutation, recombination, drift, gene flow, selection at
multiple levels) are a deep field on their own. The Modern Synthesis fused
Darwinian selection with Mendelian genetics in the early 20th century;
current debate is over how much the "Extended Evolutionary Synthesis"
(developmental plasticity, niche construction, epigenetic inheritance)
actually changes the picture versus just adding detail. Speciation,
phylogenetics, and the molecular clock are the tools for reconstructing how
any of this actually played out.

## Ecology (`BIO_ECO`)

Ecology scales evolutionary and physiological principles up to populations,
communities, and ecosystems: population growth models (logistic growth,
predator-prey dynamics), competition and niche theory, food webs, and
biogeochemical cycling of carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus. It's also the
field most directly load-bearing for climate and conservation policy, which
means a lot of ecological questions (how resilient is a given ecosystem to
a perturbation, what counts as a "healthy" population) are simultaneously
scientific and normative. Population genetics sits at the seam between this
section and statistics — allele frequency change over time is fundamentally
a statistical inference problem.
