# Ecology

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_ECO`

Populations, communities, and ecosystems — biology at the scale where
experiments are hardest and the systems are least controllable. Ecology is
the domain where prediction is genuinely difficult for structural reasons:
long timescales, no replicates, nonlinear feedbacks, and outcomes that
depend on history. Classic tidy results (competitive exclusion, trophic
cascades, island biogeography, r/K selection) turn out to be much more
context-dependent than their textbook framing suggests. Whether ecosystems
have stable equilibria at all, whether diversity causes stability or merely
correlates with it, and whether tipping points are real regime shifts or
artifacts of coarse observation are all still argued about — and these
arguments carry directly into conservation and climate policy.

## Open questions

- Does ecology have real general laws, or only local regularities that
  don't transfer between systems?
- Is the diversity-stability relationship causal, and in which direction?
- How much can any of this be tested, given that the interesting units are
  unreplicable and slow?
- Are ecosystem tipping points a genuine dynamical phenomenon or a
  narrative imposed on noisy time series after the fact?
- What is the honest confidence interval on an ecological forecast used to
  justify a policy decision?
- Where does the "balance of nature" intuition do real damage to
  ecological reasoning?
