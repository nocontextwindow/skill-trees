# Evolutionary Biology

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_EVO`

The theory that makes the rest of biology cohere, and the one most often
handled sloppily. Natural selection is one of several forces — drift,
migration, mutation, and constraint all shape populations, and in small
populations drift dominates selection entirely. Population genetics is the
quantitative core; the verbal version of evolution is where adaptationist
just-so stories breed, because almost any trait can be given a plausible
selective narrative after the fact. Genuine live disputes remain: the level
at which selection acts, how much of the genome is functional versus
neutral, whether evolution is gradual or punctuated in any deep sense,
and how much developmental constraint channels what variation is even
available to select on.

## Open questions

- How do I tell a real adaptive explanation from an unfalsifiable
  just-so story, and what evidence would distinguish them?
- At what level does selection actually operate — gene, organism, group —
  and is the disagreement substantive or a bookkeeping preference?
- How much of observed biological structure is adaptation versus drift,
  constraint, or historical accident?
- Does thinking in terms of fitness landscapes clarify anything, given
  that the landscape itself changes as populations move on it?
- Where does evolutionary reasoning legitimately extend beyond genes —
  culture, ideas, institutions — and where does the analogy break down
  because the inheritance mechanism is wrong?
- What would falsify a specific evolutionary claim, as opposed to the
  theory as a whole?
