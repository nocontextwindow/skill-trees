# Physiology & Anatomy

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_PHYS`

How organ systems work and how they're regulated — the integrative level
where molecular knowledge has to become an account of a functioning body.
The organizing idea is homeostasis via feedback control, but the actual
control systems are redundant, overlapping, and full of loops with delays,
which is why single-variable interventions so often produce unexpected
whole-system responses. Physiology is also where scaling laws bite: body
size determines metabolic rate, heart rate, lifespan, and diffusion
constraints in ways that make an elephant not a large mouse. Much of what
clinical and nutritional advice rests on is physiology inferred from
short-term biomarker changes, and the gap between "moves the marker" and
"changes the outcome" is where most of the failures live.

## Open questions

- When does a biomarker actually stand in for the outcome I care about,
  and how would I know it doesn't?
- How much of physiology is genuinely mechanistic understanding versus
  curve-fitted description that happens to work in a normal range?
- Where do the allometric scaling laws come from, and are they physical
  necessities or evolutionary regularities?
- What does homeostasis fail to explain — where is the body clearly not
  defending a setpoint?
- How should I weigh mechanistic plausibility against outcome evidence when
  they point in different directions?
- Which parts of anatomy are worth learning as structure and which are only
  meaningful as function?
