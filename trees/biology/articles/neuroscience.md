# Neuroscience

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_NEURO`

From ion channels and synapses up to circuits, systems, and behaviour —
and the field with the widest gap between data volume and explanation. We
have the complete connectome of C. elegans and still cannot fully predict
its behaviour, which is a standing warning about what wiring diagrams
deliver on their own. The levels problem is the central difficulty: an
account in terms of spikes, one in terms of circuits, and one in terms of
psychology all seem necessary and don't obviously reduce to one another.
Meanwhile methodology has been repeatedly self-correcting the hard way —
fMRI reverse inference, underpowered imaging studies, and the collapse of
simple neurotransmitter-imbalance stories about mood disorders are all
cautionary examples still cited in current practice.

## Open questions

- What would a satisfying explanation of a behaviour in neural terms even
  look like, and would I recognize one if I had it?
- Is the levels problem a temporary state of knowledge or a permanent
  feature of how brains have to be described?
- How much does a connectome buy you without dynamics, neuromodulation, and
  state?
- Where does the brain-as-computer framing earn its keep, and where does it
  quietly import assumptions that don't hold?
- What can imaging actually establish, given its temporal and spatial
  resolution — and what claims routinely outrun it?
- Does anything in neuroscience currently bear on consciousness, or are the
  correlates being mistaken for an explanation?
