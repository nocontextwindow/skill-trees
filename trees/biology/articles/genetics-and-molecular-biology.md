# Genetics & Molecular Biology

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_GENETICS`

How information is stored, expressed, and inherited: DNA, transcription,
translation, and the regulatory layers on top. The central dogma is a good
scaffold and a bad summary — the interesting biology is almost all in
regulation, splicing, non-coding RNA, chromatin state, and epigenetic
marks, none of which the simple version predicts. The genotype-phenotype
map is the hard open problem: most traits are massively polygenic, GWAS
hits are mostly regulatory variants of tiny individual effect, and heritability
estimates get read as claims about causation and modifiability that they do
not support. "A gene for X" is nearly always wrong, and knowing why it's
wrong is more useful than knowing the codon table.

## Open questions

- What does heritability actually license me to conclude, and what do
  people routinely conclude from it that it doesn't support?
- If most complex traits are thousands of tiny effects, is mechanistic
  understanding of them achievable in principle or only statistical
  prediction?
- How much of gene regulation is a program being executed and how much is a
  network settling into an attractor?
- Where does epigenetics have real explanatory weight versus where has it
  become a rhetorical placeholder for "environment matters"?
- What would it take to predict a phenotype from a genome sequence, and for
  which traits is that already essentially solved or clearly hopeless?
- Is the gene the right unit of analysis at all, or a historical artifact
  of what was measurable first?
