# Cell Biology

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_CELL`

The cell as the smallest thing that is unambiguously alive: membranes,
organelles, the cytoskeleton, trafficking, energy metabolism, and the cell
cycle. The textbook diagram — a tidy cutaway with labelled compartments —
is the main thing to unlearn. A real cell is dense, jostling, and largely
organized by transient interactions and phase-separated condensates that
have no membrane at all; molecular motors walk along filaments that are
constantly being built and torn down. Compartmentalization is the deep
theme: most of what a eukaryotic cell does is keep incompatible chemistries
apart and then move things between them, and the endosymbiotic origin of
mitochondria means part of that machinery came from a different organism
entirely.

## Open questions

- What is the actual boundary of "alive"? Are viruses excluded for a
  principled reason or a historical one?
- How much cellular organization is coded and how much is self-assembly
  that emerges from physics given the right components?
- Why did eukaryotic complexity happen apparently once, and what does that
  say about how hard the transition was?
- Is the "cell as machine" metaphor helping or hurting me — and what does
  it predict wrongly?
- How do processes running on stochastic single-molecule events produce
  reliable macroscopic outcomes?
- Where does knowing structure stop predicting function, and what fills the
  gap?
