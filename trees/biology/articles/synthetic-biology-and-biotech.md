# Synthetic Biology / Biotech

[← Biology](../index.md) · `BIO_SYNBIO`

Engineering biological systems on purpose: gene editing, metabolic
engineering, directed evolution, designed proteins, and standardized
genetic parts. The founding aspiration was to make biology an engineering
discipline with modular, composable, characterized components — and the
persistent difficulty is that biological parts are not modular. Context
dependence, metabolic burden, crosstalk, and the fact that engineered
organisms evolve away from your design are why circuits that work on a
bench rarely scale. CRISPR made editing cheap without making prediction of
downstream consequences easy, and protein structure prediction has been
largely solved while protein *function* prediction has not. The dual-use
and biosecurity questions here are not hypothetical.

## Open questions

- Can biology be made genuinely modular, or is context dependence
  irreducible and the engineering framing wrong?
- What is the real bottleneck now — design, construction, or measurement?
- How do you engineer a system that evolves against your specification, and
  is evolutionary stability a designable property?
- Given cheap editing, what actually limits therapeutic gene editing:
  delivery, off-target effects, or regulation?
- Where does the gap between predicting a protein's structure and
  predicting its behaviour in a cell actually sit?
- How should the dual-use risk be weighed against openness in a field where
  the capability is diffusing regardless?
