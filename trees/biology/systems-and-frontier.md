## Physiology & Anatomy (`BIO_PHYS`)

Physiology is the study of how organ systems maintain homeostasis —
circulation, respiration, digestion, endocrine signaling, and the
kidney/fluid balance system all being answers to the same basic problem: a
multicellular body needs to keep its internal environment stable despite a
changing external one. Anatomy provides the structural vocabulary that
makes physiology legible. Comparative physiology (how a camel's kidney
differs from a human's, how diving mammals manage oxygen) is a good way to
see which physiological solutions are contingent versus close to
physically necessary.

## Neuroscience (`BIO_NEURO`)

Neuroscience runs from the biophysics of a single neuron's action potential
up through circuits, systems (visual, motor, limbic), and the unresolved
question of how any of that produces subjective experience. The field has
genuinely useful mechanistic stories at the circuit level (e.g. how the
hippocampus supports spatial memory) but is much shakier once you ask about
consciousness or higher cognition, where competing frameworks (global
workspace theory, integrated information theory, predictive processing)
are still live and none commands consensus. Artificial neural networks were
loosely inspired by biological ones, but it's worth being precise about how
thin that analogy actually is at the mechanistic level.

## Synthetic Biology / Biotech (`BIO_SYNBIO`)

Synthetic biology treats genetic circuits as engineering substrate — parts
(promoters, genes, regulatory elements) that can be composed to make a cell
do something it wouldn't do on its own, from producing insulin to sensing
an environmental toxin. It draws directly on both molecular genetics
(BIO_GENETICS) and the systems-level view of how cells actually behave
(BIO_NEURO, BIO_PHYS) because a circuit that works on paper still has to
survive the cell's own regulatory noise and evolutionary pressure to
discard unused function. The dual-use problem is real and not hypothetical:
the same toolkit that makes gene drives possible for eradicating malaria-
carrying mosquitoes is the toolkit biosecurity researchers worry about most.
