# Naturalized Epistemology

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_NAT`

The proposal that epistemology give up trying to justify science from some
position outside it and become instead an empirical study of how creatures
actually get from stimulus to theory. The standing objection is that this
drops normativity: a description of how minds reason can't by itself say
how they should. Defenders answer that the norms come back as means-ends
claims once truth is fixed as the goal. The practically important version
is the attitude rather than the doctrine — treating your own cognition as a
system to be modeled, tested, and calibrated, rather than as a source of
self-evident deliverances.

## Open questions

- Can a purely descriptive account of cognition generate norms, or does the
  "ought" have to be imported from somewhere else?
- Epistemology would use science, and science needs epistemology. Is that
  circle vicious, or just what it looks like to have no outside?
- Does knowing the mechanism behind a belief change how much the belief
  deserves to be trusted?
- What feedback loop would it actually take to calibrate myself on the
  questions I care about — and do I have one for any of them?
- Is an accurate world model the goal, or is accuracy sometimes traded
  against usefulness in ways I should accept rather than correct?
