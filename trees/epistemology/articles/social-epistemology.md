# Social Epistemology

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_SOCIAL`

Knowledge as something produced and held collectively — through testimony,
expertise, institutions, and disagreement. Two hard cores. First, whether
testimony is a basic source or reducible to inductive evidence about
speakers, which matters because no individual could ever assemble the
inductive base. Second, peer disagreement: when someone with equal
competence and evidence believes the opposite, conciliatory views say move
toward them, steadfast views say your reasons remain your reasons. Full
conciliationism has an uncomfortable consequence — it makes belief a
function of which population you happen to sample.

## Open questions

- Should I move toward a peer who disagrees? What's the honest answer when
  I'm confident I thought about it harder?
- How do I evaluate expert claims in a field where I can't evaluate the
  arguments — and what am I actually assessing when I do?
- Is testimony a basic source of knowledge, or a shortcut licensed by
  evidence about testifiers?
- When many people agree, when is that many pieces of evidence and when is
  it one piece counted repeatedly?
- Can a group know something no member of it knows? Is a group a knower at
  all, or is that a manner of speaking?
