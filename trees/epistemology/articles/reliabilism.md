# Reliabilism

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_REL`

A belief is justified if it was produced by a process that reliably yields
truths — justification becomes a fact about mechanisms rather than about
reasons the believer can cite. This handles animals, small children, and
ordinary perception gracefully, and it explains what goes wrong in Gettier
cases without inventing a fourth condition. Its hardest problem is
generality: any belief is the output of processes describable at many
levels of grain, reliability varies enormously with the description chosen,
and there's no principled rule for picking the right one. It also concedes
that someone can be justified while having no idea why — which is either
the whole point or the decisive objection.

## Open questions

- Is the generality problem fatal to the view, or merely unsolved?
- Reliable in which world — this one, or nearby ones? Is there a fact of
  the matter about which reference class counts?
- Can a process be reliable while the believer is epistemically reckless?
  Which of the two do I actually care about?
- Does externalism explain justification, or quietly change the subject
  from justification to something else?
- Which of my own belief-forming processes have I ever actually checked
  for reliability, and how would I check the rest?
