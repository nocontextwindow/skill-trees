# Philosophy of Language

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_LANG`

Language sits under epistemology because what can be known is bounded by
what can be said and thought. The core disputes are about reference (does
a word pick out its object through a description in the speaker's head, or
through a causal chain and social deference the speaker knows nothing
about?) and about whether there are truths knowable by meaning alone. That
second one is directly an epistemological question: the attack on the
analytic/synthetic distinction removes the category of things known without
looking, and with it a large part of what rationalism had claimed.

## Open questions

- Is there a real analytic/synthetic distinction, or is every statement
  revisable under enough pressure from the rest of the web?
- Does what's in a speaker's head fix what their words refer to? If not,
  in what sense does the speaker know what they're talking about?
- Is vagueness a feature of language, of our knowledge, or of the world?
- How much of what looks like substantive disagreement is disagreement
  about word use — and what test distinguishes the two?
- Can I think something I have no words for? Does the answer constrain
  what I can know?
