# Theories of Justification

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_JUST`

The regress problem generates the entire landscape: a belief justified by
another belief needs that one justified too, and the chain must terminate,
loop back, run on forever, or not be a chain at all. Foundationalism,
coherentism, infinitism, and externalism are the four exits. Cutting across
all of them is the internalism/externalism split — whether what justifies a
belief must be accessible to the believer, or whether justification can be
a fact about the belief's causal provenance that the believer has no view
of. Underneath both is a question rarely asked directly: what justification
is *for*, whether it tracks truth or tracks responsible believing, and what
happens when those diverge.

## Open questions

- Must the justifiers of a belief be accessible to the believer? What is
  lost on each answer?
- Is justification aimed at truth or at blameless believing — and what do
  I say about a case where they come apart?
- Is the regress a genuine problem, or an artifact of modeling
  justification as a linear chain of supports?
- Is talk of what I *ought* to believe coherent, given that belief isn't
  voluntary?
- Are there beliefs I can't justify but shouldn't drop? What does that fact
  tell me about the theory rather than about the beliefs?
