# Coherentism

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_COH`

Justification is a property of a system of beliefs rather than a relation
to something outside it: a belief is justified by how it fits. The standing
objection is isolation — a perfectly coherent fiction is perfectly
coherent — and the standard repair lets experience constrain the system,
at which point coherentism starts to resemble a foundationalism with soft
foundations. There is also an unsolved technical problem at the center:
coherence has to be more than consistency and less than mutual entailment,
and no formal account of it has proven satisfying.

## Open questions

- What is coherence, precisely? Is there an account that isn't circular or
  merely metaphorical?
- Can a coherent system be constrained by experience without smuggling
  foundations back in?
- Why should coherence be truth-conducive at all? Well-built fiction
  coheres.
- Is coherentism a theory of justification, or a theory of belief revision
  that has been mislabeled as one?
- My beliefs do form a web. Does anything in it actually play a
  foundational role, or does everything just hold everything else up?
