# Sources of Knowledge

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_SOURCES`

Perception, reason, memory, testimony, introspection. The standard picture
ranks these, treating perception and reason as basic and testimony as
derivative — but nearly everything any person believes arrived by
testimony, so the ranking is at odds with the actual structure of anyone's
belief set. Each source also has a distinctive failure mode: perception may
be theory-laden all the way down, memory can only ever be checked against
other memories, and introspection is demonstrably poor at reporting the
causes of one's own judgments. None of them can be validated without
eventually relying on itself or on another member of the set.

## Open questions

- Is any source more basic than the others, or do they only ever support
  each other in a circle?
- Almost everything I believe came from testimony. Is that a defect to be
  minimized or the normal and unavoidable condition of knowledge?
- Can a source vouch for itself non-circularly? If not, what's the status
  of trusting it anyway?
- Is perception theory-laden all the way down, or is there a layer that's
  simply given?
- How much should I trust introspective reports about my own reasons,
  given how badly they perform under experiment?
