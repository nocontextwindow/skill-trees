# Skepticism

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_SKEPT`

The serious skeptical arguments aren't demands for certainty; they're
arguments that ordinary justification is structurally insufficient. The
closure argument is the sharpest: if knowing something entails knowing what
it obviously implies, and I don't know I'm not radically deceived, then I
don't know the ordinary things either. Every response is expensive —
denying closure, making knowledge attributions context-sensitive, or
refusing the conclusion on the grounds that the ordinary belief is more
credible than any premise arguing against it. The real question may be
whether skepticism is a problem to be solved or a reductio of the standard
that generates it.

## Open questions

- Which is more credible: a valid skeptical argument, or the ordinary
  belief it denies? What licenses that comparison in either direction?
- Does knowledge really close under known entailment? What's the cost of
  denying it?
- Can the regress of justification stop anywhere non-arbitrary?
- Is skepticism a live threat, or a signal that the concept of knowledge
  has been loaded with more than it can carry?
- If nothing I do changes whether global skepticism is true, is that an
  answer to it or an evasion of it?
