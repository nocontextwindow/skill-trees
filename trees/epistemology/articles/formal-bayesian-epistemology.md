# Formal / Bayesian Epistemology

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_BAYES`

Model belief as degrees rather than as an on/off state, require those
degrees to obey the probability axioms, and update by conditionalization.
The coherence requirement has real arguments behind it — incoherent
credences are exploitable, and are dominated in accuracy by coherent ones.
What the framework does not settle is where priors come from, and whether
any constraint on them beyond coherence can be defended. It also assumes
logical omniscience and a fixed hypothesis space, which is precisely what
real inquiry lacks: most of the work is generating hypotheses, and
conditionalization has nothing to say about that.

## Open questions

- Where do priors come from, and is there any defensible constraint on them
  beyond coherence?
- Does the Dutch book argument show that incoherence is irrational, or only
  that it's exploitable by someone who wants to?
- How should an agent handle a hypothesis it has not yet thought of? Is
  there a principled move, or only patches?
- Is credence the right primitive, or does full belief do work that degrees
  can't capture — in assertion, in action, in reasoning?
- Where does the Bayesian picture break down in my own reasoning, and do I
  want to repair it or route around it?
