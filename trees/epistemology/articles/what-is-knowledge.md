# What Is Knowledge?

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_INTRO`

The classical analysis — knowledge is justified true belief — is the
standard starting point and almost certainly isn't sufficient. But the
deeper question is whether knowledge admits of an analysis at all: whether
it's a concept assembled from simpler parts, or a primitive that resists
decomposition. Knowledge-first epistemology inverts the whole project,
taking knowing as basic and defining evidence and belief in terms of it.
Prior to any of that sits a question about method: what an analysis is even
answerable to — ordinary usage, intuitions about cases, or a job we've
decided we want the concept to do.

## Open questions

- Is knowledge decomposable into conditions, or is the search for necessary
  and sufficient conditions a mistake about what kind of concept it is?
- What is a theory of knowledge answerable to? If intuitions about cases
  are the data, why are they good data?
- Does knowledge come in degrees, or is it a threshold on something
  (confidence, evidence, reliability) that does?
- Truth is a condition on knowledge but never a condition I can check from
  the inside. What work is it doing in the analysis, then?
- Do I need the concept of knowledge at all, or would well-calibrated
  belief do everything I actually want from it?
