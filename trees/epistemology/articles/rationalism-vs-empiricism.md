# Rationalism vs Empiricism

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_RATEMP`

Whether any substantive knowledge is available prior to experience. The
caricature version is dead — nobody denies that experience matters or that
we reason. The live question is whether there is synthetic a priori
knowledge: content about the world that can be known without checking. What
forces the issue is mathematics and logic, which don't look empirical and
don't look empty either. Kant restructures the question by making the a
priori a fact about the conditions of experience; the Quinean line
dissolves the category entirely. Separately, cognitive science has
established that minds come with substantial innate structure — which is
either rationalism vindicated or just biology, depending on whether
built-in machinery counts as knowledge.

## Open questions

- Is there synthetic a priori knowledge, or is every apparently a priori
  truth either empty or empirically revisable?
- What is mathematical knowledge knowledge *of*, and how could experience
  bear on it either way?
- Does innate cognitive structure count as a priori knowledge, or only as a
  mechanism that reliably produces beliefs?
- If experience is the sole source, what justifies the inference rules used
  to get from experience to any conclusion?
- Where do my own strongest convictions actually come from — and does
  tracing them change how much weight they deserve?
