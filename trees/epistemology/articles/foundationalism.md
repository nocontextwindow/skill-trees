# Foundationalism

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_FOUND`

Some beliefs are justified non-inferentially, and everything else rests on
them. The structural dilemma is sharp: basic beliefs have to be secure
enough to stop the regress and contentful enough to support the
superstructure, and these pull in opposite directions. Make them reports of
immediate experience and you buy security at the cost of a gap between them
and the world; make them about the world and they need justification like
anything else. The "myth of the given" objection presses exactly here — the
claim that nothing can both have enough content to justify a belief and be
exempt from needing justification itself.

## Open questions

- Can anything be both self-justifying and informative?
- Can a non-propositional experience justify a proposition at all, or only
  cause a belief in one?
- If basic beliefs are merely *more* secure rather than certain, does the
  structure still do the job it was built for?
- Are foundations discovered or chosen? What makes one starting point
  better than another if neither is inferred?
- If I trace one of my own beliefs all the way down, what's actually at the
  bottom — and does it look like a foundation or like a habit?
