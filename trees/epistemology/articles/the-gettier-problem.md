# The Gettier Problem

[← Epistemology](../index.md) · `EP_GETTIER`

Cases where a belief is true, justified, and true only by luck — the
justification and the thing that makes the belief true have come apart. The
striking part isn't the original counterexamples but the record since:
every proposed fourth condition (no false lemmas, defeasibility,
sensitivity, safety) has generated fresh counterexamples, for over half a
century. That pattern is itself evidence, and the question is evidence of
what — that knowledge is unanalyzable, that the concept is irreducibly
vague, or that intuitions about elaborately contrived cases were never
reliable data in the first place.

## Open questions

- Is the persistent failure to patch the analysis evidence that the project
  is misconceived, or just that the right condition hasn't been found?
- What exactly does epistemic luck rule out? Is there a principled account
  of "true for the right reason" that isn't circular?
- Should intuitions about artificial cases carry evidential weight at all?
- Would a strong enough justification condition close the gap by itself, so
  that the real lesson is that justification was set too cheap?
- Does anything downstream actually change depending on whether Gettier
  cases count as knowledge?
