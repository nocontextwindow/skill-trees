## Thermodynamics of Materials (`MAT_THERMO`)

Phase diagrams are thermodynamics made visual: they tell you, for a given
composition and temperature, which phases are stable and in what
proportion, and they're derived from the same Gibbs free energy
minimization that governs any chemical system. Phase transformations
(solidification, precipitation, eutectoid reactions like the one that makes
steel heat-treatable) are the mechanism by which processing turns a fixed
composition into wildly different microstructures and properties. Diffusion
(Fick's laws) sets the kinetics — how fast a transformation can actually
happen — which is why heat treatment is as much about time and cooling rate
as it is about temperature.

## Mechanical Properties of Materials (`MAT_MECH`)

The stress-strain curve is the central object here: elastic modulus, yield
strength, ultimate tensile strength, and ductility are all read off of it,
and understanding why the curve bends the way it does (dislocation motion,
work hardening, necking) connects straight back to crystal structure and
defects. Beyond simple tension, real components fail through fatigue
(cyclic loading well below yield strength), creep (slow deformation under
sustained load at high temperature), and fracture (crack propagation,
governed by fracture mechanics rather than simple stress limits). This
section is the direct scientific basis for civil and mechanical
engineering's structural design work.
