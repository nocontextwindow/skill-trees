# Electronic & Semiconductor Materials

[← Materials Science](../index.md) · `MAT_ELEC`

Band structure, doping, junctions, and the materials that make electronics
possible. This is the branch where quantum mechanics is not optional —
band theory is genuinely required rather than decorative — and where
purity requirements are extreme in a way no other materials field
demands: parts-per-billion contamination changes device behaviour, and
defect engineering is done at the level of individual dopant atoms. The
striking historical fact is that silicon won not because it is the best
semiconductor but because SiO2 is an excellent, easily grown native oxide,
which is a lesson about how processability beats intrinsic properties.
Dimensional scaling has now hit physical limits, so the interesting
questions have moved to materials and architecture rather than
lithography alone.

## Open questions

- How much band theory do I need to reason usefully about devices, and
  where does the semiclassical picture stop being adequate?
- Why did processability beat performance in silicon's case, and where else
  does that pattern hold?
- What actually limits scaling now — physics, heat, economics, or
  manufacturing yield — and are those separable?
- Is the search for a silicon successor a materials problem or an
  ecosystem-lock-in problem?
- Where do wide-bandgap materials genuinely change the design space rather
  than just improve a number?
- How much of device behaviour is bulk property and how much is interface
  and defect state?
