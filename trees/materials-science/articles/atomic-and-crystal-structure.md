# Atomic & Crystal Structure

[← Materials Science](../index.md) · `MAT_STRUCT`

Lattices, unit cells, symmetry, and — much more importantly — defects. The
core insight of the field is that the perfect crystal is a useful fiction
and essentially all interesting mechanical behaviour comes from what's
wrong with it: vacancies, dislocations, grain boundaries, precipitates.
Dislocation motion is why metals yield at a fraction of their theoretical
strength, and every strengthening mechanism ever devised is some way of
making dislocations harder to move. Amorphous and glassy materials sit
awkwardly outside this whole picture, and characterization is its own
problem: diffraction gives you an averaged reciprocal-space answer that has
to be inverted into a real-space structure, which is not a neutral step.

## Open questions

- If defects control behaviour, why is so much of the teaching organized
  around perfect lattices — and does that ordering cost anything?
- How much of a material's history is readable from its microstructure, and
  where does that inference become guesswork?
- What is the right level of description for a glass, given that "structure"
  in the crystallographic sense doesn't apply?
- Which characterization technique answers which question, and where do I
  risk reading an artifact of the method as a feature of the material?
- Is there a principled way to know which length scale controls a given
  property, or is it always empirical?
- Can microstructure be designed forward from a target property, or only
  discovered by iterating processing?
