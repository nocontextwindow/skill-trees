# Chemistry Foundations

[← Materials Science](../index.md) · `MAT_CHEM`

Bonding, periodicity, and reaction chemistry as the substrate for material
properties. The useful frame here is that the four bonding types — metallic,
ionic, covalent, van der Waals — are idealizations, and nearly every real
material sits somewhere between them; electronegativity difference is a
continuum, not a classifier. Bonding character sets the ceiling on what a
material can do (melting point, stiffness, conductivity, brittleness), but
almost never the actual value, because defects and microstructure dominate
real behaviour. Surface and interface chemistry is the part that is
routinely underweighted and then turns out to control corrosion, adhesion,
catalysis, and most failures that start from the outside in.

## Open questions

- How far does bonding type actually get me in predicting a property, and
  where does the prediction hand off to structure?
- Why is the theoretical strength of a material typically 10–100x its real
  strength, and what does that gap tell me about where to look?
- Which properties are genuinely intrinsic to a chemistry and which are
  processing artifacts I could change?
- When is a surface effectively a different material from the bulk, and how
  would I notice?
- How much quantum mechanics is load-bearing for materials intuition versus
  a formalism I can safely treat as a black box?
- Where do periodic-table heuristics still work, and where has computational
  screening made them obsolete?
