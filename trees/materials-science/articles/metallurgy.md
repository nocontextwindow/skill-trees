# Metallurgy

[← Materials Science](../index.md) · `MAT_METALS`

Metals and alloys: the class where processing-structure-property-performance
is best understood and most directly manipulable. Heat treatment is the
central lever — the same composition of steel can be soft and ductile or
hard and brittle depending entirely on thermal history, which is a striking
demonstration that composition is not destiny. Strengthening mechanisms
(solid solution, precipitation, work hardening, grain refinement) are well
catalogued and mostly additive, but almost all of them cost ductility, with
grain refinement the notable partial exception. Metallurgy is also the
oldest engineering discipline and carries a lot of empirical craft
knowledge that predates and still outruns its theory, and additive
manufacturing has recently invalidated a good deal of accumulated intuition
by producing microstructures conventional processing never made.

## Open questions

- How much of metallurgical practice is derived from theory versus
  empirically found and later rationalized — and does the difference matter
  for trusting it?
- Why does grain refinement improve strength and toughness together when
  nearly every other mechanism trades them off?
- What does additive manufacturing actually break about conventional
  metallurgical intuition, and what survives?
- Can alloy design be done predictively now, or is high-throughput search
  still doing the real work?
- Where does corrosion belong — a materials property, a design problem, or
  an environment problem — and how does that framing change decisions?
- How much does recyclability and impurity accumulation constrain what
  alloys are actually usable at scale?
