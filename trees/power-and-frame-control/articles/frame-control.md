# Frame Control Fundamentals

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_FRAME`

A frame is the implicit definition of what a situation *is* — what's being
discussed, who has standing to speak, what would count as a good reason.
Whoever's definition gets used has settled most arguments before they're
made, which is well attested in the research on framing effects,
agenda-setting, and anchoring in negotiation. The popular literature
overclaims badly, treating frame control as a stack of dominance tricks; the
underlying phenomenon is real but mostly unglamorous, since frames are
usually set by whoever names the situation first and then held by everyone
tacitly accepting the name. There's an ethical fork built into the skill:
refusing a frame someone is imposing on you and imposing one on someone
else use the same competence and are not the same act.

## Open questions

- What actually determines whose frame holds — confidence, standing,
  moving first, or the audience's own interests?
- Is refusing a frame different in kind from imposing one, or only in
  direction?
- Can you hold a frame under real pressure without hardening into someone
  who can no longer update?
- When is accepting an unfavourable frame the correct move?
- What does frame control look like once the other party has noticed you're
  doing it?
