# Supremacy & Dominance Dynamics

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_DOMINANCE`

What happens when one party's advantage grows large enough that it no
longer needs anyone's cooperation. Dominance in that strong sense removes
the ordinary incentives for restraint, so the interesting question is what
remains to constrain it — internal norms, fear of eventual reversal, or
simply the cost of enforcement exceeding the benefit. Historically,
near-total dominance tends to be self-limiting through over-extension,
counter-coalitions, and internal decay, but "tends to" is carrying an
enormous amount of weight there and the exceptions are the cases that
matter. The same structure appears at personal scale, where being clearly
the strongest party in a group produces the identical constraint problem,
and the failure mode looks like a slow, unnoticed drift in what seems
acceptable.

## Open questions

- What actually constrains a party that no longer needs anyone's
  cooperation?
- Is the historical self-limitation of dominant powers a real regularity,
  or pattern-matching by the survivors?
- Does holding a large advantage change the holder — and what would you
  have to build in advance to notice it happening to you?
- What is owed to a party you could simply overrule at no cost?
- Is there a stable form of dominance, or is stability itself the thing
  that always eventually fails?
