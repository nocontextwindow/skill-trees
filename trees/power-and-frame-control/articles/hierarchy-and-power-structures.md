# Hierarchy & Power Structures

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_HIER`

Why hierarchies form, how they distribute authority, and what keeps them
standing. They appear in every group past a certain size, which supports a
functional account rooted in coordination costs — though the fact that
*some* hierarchy is functional says nothing about whether any particular
one is justified. Formal structure and actual influence routinely diverge:
the org chart and the map of who really has to sign off are different
documents, and misreading which one you're operating in is a common and
expensive error. Stability comes far less from force than from the
widespread belief that the arrangement is normal, which is why legitimacy
is a strategic asset and not just a moral nicety.

## Open questions

- Is hierarchy inevitable past some group size, or only under particular
  incentive structures?
- How do you read the informal structure when everyone in it has an
  interest in misrepresenting it?
- What makes a hierarchy legitimate to the people inside it, and is that
  ever the same thing as being legitimate?
- Does flattening a hierarchy remove it, or just make it harder to see and
  harder to appeal?
- Where does a hierarchy's authority actually reside, given that the person
  at the top can be replaced without it collapsing?
