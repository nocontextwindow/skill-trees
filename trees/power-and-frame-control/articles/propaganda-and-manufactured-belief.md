# Propaganda & Manufactured Belief

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_PROP`

The deliberate shaping of mass belief. What replicates is unglamorous:
repetition raises perceived truth regardless of content, source credibility
and framing move opinion, and prior belief filters everything that arrives.
The strong "brainwashing" picture — that people can be reprogrammed against
their own interests — is far weaker than its cultural reputation; the
mid-century research that gave the word its currency found remarkably few
durable conversions. Modern forms appear to work less by installing beliefs
than by controlling salience, flooding the field until nothing can be
evaluated, and making certain positions socially expensive to hold. The
awkward part is that the same techniques describe advertising, education,
and any culture reproducing itself, so the definition of "propaganda" is
quietly doing a lot of the moral work.

## Open questions

- What distinguishes propaganda from persuasion, education, and culture —
  intent, method, truth, or nothing that survives examination?
- If repetition moves belief regardless of content, is there any defence
  that isn't just controlling your own exposure?
- Is the modern form better modelled as attention control than belief
  control?
- Can you make yourself resistant, or does knowing a mechanism fail to stop
  it working on you?
- What is the honest version of transmitting values to people not yet able
  to evaluate them?
