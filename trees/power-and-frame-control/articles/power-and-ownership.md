# Power & Ownership

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_OWNER`

Ownership is the right to decide about a thing and to exclude others from
deciding — control, not mere possession. Power concentrates in ownership
because of that residual right: whoever owns an asset sets the terms for
everyone who needs it, which makes the line between owning and operating
one of the sharpest divisions in how power actually distributes. Modern
forms complicate the picture, since control can be separated from title
through governance rights, covenants, and dependency, so nominal owners
sometimes hold very little real say and non-owners sometimes hold a great
deal. The question worth working out is which forms of ownership are power
and which are merely claims on a stream of money.

## Open questions

- What is the real difference between owning something and controlling it,
  and which of the two do you actually want?
- When does dependency on an asset give its owner more power than the law
  ever granted them?
- Is ownership a natural right, a legal convention, or a description of who
  can enforce exclusion?
- What can be owned that shouldn't be, and how would you defend that line
  without appealing to intuition?
- Why does having a claim on the upside change people's behaviour so much
  more than simply being paid well?
