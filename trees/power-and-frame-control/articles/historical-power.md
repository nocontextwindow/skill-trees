# Historical Power

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_HISTORY`

How power was actually built, held, and lost at civilizational scale —
conquest, colonization, state formation, empire, succession. The value of
studying it is that history is the only real dataset on the question, and
the recurring mechanics (extraction, administration, legitimation, elite
settlement, the succession problem) appear across cultures with no contact
between them. The trap is that the record is shaped twice: once by who
survived to write it, and again by the present-day arguments the past keeps
getting conscripted into. The hardest interpretive question, and the one
everything else depends on, is how much of any outcome is structural —
geography, disease, technology, institutions — and how much turns on the
decisions of a handful of people.

## Open questions

- How much of large-scale historical outcome is structural, and how much is
  contingent on individuals?
- What actually holds an empire together after the conquest phase, and why
  does that mechanism always eventually fail?
- Which mechanics of historical power still operate, and which were
  artifacts of a particular technology?
- How do you read a source knowing that both its writer and you have a
  stake in the conclusion?
- Does the moral evaluation of a historical order tell you anything useful
  about how it actually worked?
