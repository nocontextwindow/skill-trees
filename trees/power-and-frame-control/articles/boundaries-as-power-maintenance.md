# Boundaries as Power Maintenance

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_BOUND`

A boundary is a stated limit plus a demonstrated willingness to enforce it;
without the second part it is a request. The therapeutic framing presents
boundaries as self-care, and the power framing sees them as the mechanism
by which the terms of a relationship actually get set — what you tolerate
becomes the baseline everyone else plans around. Both readings are partly
right and they come apart at exactly the point where enforcing a limit
costs the relationship. The difficult part is not naming limits but paying
the enforcement cost consistently: a boundary enforced unevenly teaches
people to test it rather than respect it, which is worse than never having
stated it.

## Open questions

- What separates a boundary from a demand — the content, or who bears the
  cost of it?
- Are boundaries about the other person at all, or about not being
  perpetually renegotiated?
- What does it cost to enforce every stated limit, and is there a
  defensible version of letting some slide?
- Can someone with very few needs have boundaries, or is the concept
  parasitic on wanting things from people?
- How do you tell a boundary from rigidity that has been dressed up as
  principle?
