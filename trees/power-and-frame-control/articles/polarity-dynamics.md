# Polarity Dynamics

[← Power & Frame Control](../index.md) · `PWR_POLARITY`

The claim that attraction and complementary dynamics between people run
partly on a polarity of stances — direction and receptivity, initiation and
response — usually coded as masculine and feminine. The empirical footing
is weak and should be held loosely: most of the popular material is folk
theory assembled from anecdote, the sex-difference findings it invokes are
typically small effects across heavily overlapping distributions, and
whether the causal story is biological, cultural, or self-fulfilling is
unsettled. What *is* better attested is narrower and ungendered:
interpersonal complementarity is a real, replicated pattern — dominant
behaviour invites submissive response, warmth invites warmth — and it
doesn't need a polarity framework to describe it. The live question is
whether the gendered version adds anything the general one doesn't.

## Open questions

- Is there anything in polarity that isn't already covered by general
  interpersonal complementarity?
- How much of the observed pattern is script — real because everyone is
  following it — and does being a script make it less real?
- What is the strongest version of the case, stated without appeal to
  nature or tradition as authority?
- If the dynamic is largely learned, is deliberately performing it
  dishonest, or is that just what social roles are?
- What does this framework get wrong badly enough to cost someone
  something that mattered?
