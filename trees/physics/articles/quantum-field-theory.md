# Quantum Field Theory

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_QFT`

What you get when quantum mechanics is made compatible with special
relativity: particles become excitations of fields, particle number stops
being conserved, and antimatter is forced rather than added. It is the most
predictively accurate framework ever constructed and its mathematical
foundations are unfinished. Renormalization was widely regarded as a
swindle until the effective-field-theory reading recast it as the statement
that low-energy physics simply doesn't need to know what happens at short
distances — which is either a deep insight or a very good excuse. Whether
"particle" survives as a concept at all in curved spacetime is genuinely
disputed.

## Open questions

- Is a particle a fundamental thing here, or an artifact of one particular
  way of splitting a field into modes?
- Does the effective-field-theory reading dissolve the renormalization
  problem, or relocate it somewhere less visible?
- Why should a theory this successful lack rigorous foundations? Is rigor
  optional here, or overdue?
- Is the field the real entity, or the algebra of observables, or is the
  question itself malformed?
- What does it mean that the vacuum has structure — is empty space a thing
  with properties?
