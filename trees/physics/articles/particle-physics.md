# Particle Physics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_PARTICLE`

The Standard Model is a very specific answer: a particular gauge group,
three generations, and roughly nineteen free parameters put in by hand. It
fits essentially all accelerator data and explains none of its own
structure. Naturalness — the expectation that dimensionless parameters
shouldn't be finely tuned — drove a generation of predictions about what
the LHC would find, and those predictions largely failed, leaving the field
openly unsure whether naturalness was a real guide or an aesthetic
preference misread as one. Neutrino masses, dark matter, and the
matter-antimatter asymmetry are the only firm evidence that anything lies
beyond it.

## Open questions

- Was naturalness a real principle or an aesthetic dressed as one? If it
  fails, what replaces it as a guide?
- Are the free parameters brute facts, dynamically fixed, or selected
  anthropically — and is the last of those an explanation at all?
- Why three generations? Is "why" even well-formed here, or is that the
  wrong kind of question to ask of a fundamental theory?
- What would count as understanding the Standard Model, over and above
  being able to compute with it?
- If the next generation of experiments found nothing new, what would the
  honest conclusion be?
