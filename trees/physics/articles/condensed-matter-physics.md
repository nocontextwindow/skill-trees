# Condensed Matter Physics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_COND`

The field where "more is different" is a working principle rather than a
slogan. Superconductivity, phase transitions, and topological order are
properties of collective organization, and are often almost entirely
insensitive to the microscopic details of what's organizing. Universality
and the renormalization group emerged here and then went on to reshape
particle physics. Emergent quasiparticles behave like fundamental particles
in every operational respect, which puts real pressure on what
"fundamental" is supposed to mean — and raises the possibility that the
theories currently called fundamental are themselves effective descriptions
of something else.

## Open questions

- If emergent quasiparticles are operationally indistinguishable from
  fundamental ones, what makes anything fundamental?
- Why does universality work — why do microscopically unrelated systems
  share critical behavior exactly?
- Is reduction to microphysics true but useless here, or false in some
  defensible sense?
- Are there emergent phenomena underivable in principle from the
  microscopic laws, or only ones we can't derive in practice?
- Should this change how I read fundamental physics — is spacetime itself
  likely to be this kind of thing?
