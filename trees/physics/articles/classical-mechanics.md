# Classical Mechanics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_CLASS`

Not merely the historical starting point — the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian
reformulations are where the conceptual content lives. Newton's laws are
one formulation, the principle of least action is another, they agree
exactly on trajectories, and they suggest entirely different pictures of
what the world is like. Noether's theorem, tying every continuous symmetry
to a conservation law, appears here and is arguably the single most
important structural fact in physics. Classical mechanics is also not tame:
it contains chaos, and it's the first place where determinism and
predictability visibly come apart.

## Open questions

- Is the principle of least action a computational device or a statement
  about how nature works?
- What is the symmetry-conservation link actually telling us — is it
  explanatory, or is it definitional once you look closely?
- What does determinism buy if the system is chaotic and initial conditions
  can only ever be specified to finite precision?
- Which formulation should I take as fundamental, and is there a fact of
  the matter or only a preference?
- What is a force, once mechanics has been written without ever mentioning
  one?
