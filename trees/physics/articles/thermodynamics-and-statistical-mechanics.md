# Thermodynamics & Statistical Mechanics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_THERMO`

The one place a fundamental asymmetry in time appears in physics, and
nobody agrees where it comes from. The microscopic laws are
time-reversible; the second law is not. The standard reconstruction gets
irreversibility from coarse-graining plus an extremely low-entropy initial
condition — which relocates the puzzle to why the early universe was like
that, and raises the harder question of whether entropy is a property of a
system or of the description we impose on it. Meanwhile thermodynamics
itself is the most robust theory in physics: it survived every revolution
that destroyed the foundations it was supposedly resting on.

## Open questions

- Is entropy a fact about a system, or about an observer's coarse-graining
  of it?
- Where does the arrow of time come from — the dynamics, the initial
  conditions, or our own position inside the process?
- Why is thermodynamics so much more robust than the microphysics it is
  supposedly reducible to?
- Is the second law a law, or a claim about overwhelming probability — and
  does the difference have consequences?
- What is temperature for a system that isn't in equilibrium, and how much
  of the theory survives out there?
