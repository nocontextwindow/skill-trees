# Special Relativity

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_SR`

Two postulates, easy mathematics, and difficulty that is entirely
conceptual. The load-bearing result is the relativity of simultaneity:
whether two distant events happen at the same time depends on the frame, so
"what is happening now, elsewhere" is not a fact about the world. This is
the most metaphysically loaded result in physics and is routinely
under-weighted. The block-universe reading follows fairly directly, and
views on which only the present is real have to work hard to survive it.
The reframing of spacetime as a geometry with an invariant interval, rather
than a stage on which things happen, is the other half.

## Open questions

- If simultaneity is frame-relative, is there any defensible sense in which
  only the present is real?
- Is the block universe an implication of relativity, or an interpretation
  that relativity merely permits?
- Is the invariant interval the real physical structure, with space and
  time as projections of it?
- What is a frame — a physical situation or a coordinate choice? Does the
  distinction matter for any result?
- Why does one speed have this role at all? Is c about light, or about the
  structure of spacetime with light as an incidental example?
