# Quantum Mechanics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_QM`

The formalism is unambiguous and predicts to absurd precision; what it says
about the world is unresolved after a century. The measurement problem is
the knot: evolution is linear, deterministic, and produces superpositions,
while measurements produce single outcomes with probabilities, and both
can't be the whole story. Bell's theorem removed the comfortable escape —
no local theory assigning definite pre-existing values reproduces the
predictions — so something has to be surrendered: locality, definite
values, single outcomes, or the independence of measurement settings from
the system. Every interpretation is a different choice about which.

## Open questions

- Which of Bell's assumptions am I most willing to give up, and what does
  that commit me to everywhere else?
- Is the wavefunction a thing in the world or a state of knowledge? Is
  there any test that would separate them?
- Does the measurement problem have empirical content, or is it a demand
  for a picture the theory never promised?
- Does decoherence solve the measurement problem, or explain why it's hard
  to see while leaving it exactly where it was?
- Is the randomness fundamental, or the appearance of something
  deterministic viewed from inside it?
