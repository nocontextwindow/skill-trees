# Math Prerequisites

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_MATH`

Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations first; differential
geometry and group theory later. The formalism isn't a neutral container —
choosing to write mechanics as a variational principle rather than as
forces changes what's visible in it, and the same handful of equations
recurring across physically unrelated domains is a fact that wants
explaining rather than noting. Worth registering early: physicists' math is
often not the mathematician's. Delta functions, path integrals, and
divergent series produced correct results for decades before anyone could
say what they were, and in some cases still no one can.

## Open questions

- Why does the same small set of equations describe unrelated phenomena?
  Is that a fact about the world or about which problems are tractable?
- Does the choice of formalism ever do physical work, or is it always just
  convenience with the same content underneath?
- Physicists get right answers from math that isn't justified. What is that
  telling us — about the math, or about what justification is for?
- How much rigor is enough, and how would I notice if I'd gone past the
  point where more of it stops paying?
- Is math the language of physics, or its source? Where does the analogy
  stop being an analogy?
