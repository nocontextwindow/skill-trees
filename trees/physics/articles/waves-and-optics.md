# Waves & Optics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_WAVES`

Where the machinery that later carries quantum mechanics gets built:
superposition, interference, normal modes, Fourier decomposition. The deep
point is that wave behavior is a property of a class of equations, not of
any particular medium — which is why identical mathematics describes sound,
light, and probability amplitudes with nothing physical in common. Optics
also hosts the first variational principle, in a form that looks openly
teleological, and the first serious encounter with limits on what any
measurement can resolve.

## Open questions

- Is Fourier decomposition a physical fact about waves, or a choice of
  basis that happens to be convenient?
- Why do wave equations govern systems with nothing physically in common?
- Fermat's principle has light "choosing" the fastest path. What's the
  correct way to read that without either mysticism or dismissal?
- The position/wavenumber uncertainty relation exists before any quantum
  mechanics. What is its physical content there?
- Where does a ray description stop and a wave description start? Is that a
  real boundary or only a limit?
