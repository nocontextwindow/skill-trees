# General Relativity

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_GR`

Gravity as geometry: freely falling bodies follow straight lines through a
curved spacetime, so gravity stops being a force and becomes a property of
the stage. The equivalence principle is the physical core; the field
equations are the technical difficulty. What remains genuinely contested is
whether spacetime is a substance or only a structure of relations among
events — the hole argument makes this sharp and it has never been settled.
Two further oddities: gravitational energy cannot be localized, and the
theory reliably predicts singularities, which is to say it predicts its own
breakdown.

## Open questions

- Is spacetime a thing that exists, or the pattern of relations among
  events? What does the hole argument actually establish either way?
- Gravitational energy can't be localized. Is that a defect of the theory
  or a clue about what energy is?
- The theory predicts regions where it stops applying. Is that a failure,
  or the most informative thing it says?
- Does "gravity is not a force" survive translation into other
  formulations, or is it an artifact of this one?
- What would count as evidence about the nature of spacetime, as opposed to
  evidence about the dynamics playing out on it?
