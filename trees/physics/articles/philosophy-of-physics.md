# Philosophy of Physics

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_PHILO`

Where the interpretive questions working physics is entitled to defer get
taken up directly. Two clusters dominate: the nature of space and time —
substance or relation, whether the present is real, whether time is
fundamental at all, given that several approaches to quantum gravity have
it disappear — and the interpretation of quantum mechanics. Underneath both
is a methodological problem. Empirically equivalent formulations exist,
choosing among them requires criteria that aren't empirical, and it isn't
clear what licenses those criteria or whether using them is physics at all.

## Open questions

- When two formulations make identical predictions, is there a fact about
  which is correct? What could make one better than the other?
- Is time fundamental or an emergent parameter — and if emergent, emergent
  from what?
- What is a law of nature: a necessity, a regularity, or the best
  compression of the mosaic of particular facts?
- How much interpretive weight can a formalism carry before I'm doing
  metaphysics on borrowed authority?
- Which of my metaphysical commitments would actually be revised by a
  physics result, and which are immune no matter what turns up?
