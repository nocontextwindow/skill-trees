# Cosmology

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_COSMO`

The standard model of cosmology fits the data using two components that
have only ever been detected gravitationally and are otherwise
unidentified — dark matter and dark energy, together roughly 95% of the
contents of the universe. Inflation resolves the horizon and flatness
problems elegantly, but it's a framework rather than a single theory,
flexible enough that critics argue it can accommodate almost any
observation, and it tends to imply eternal inflation and a multiverse,
which reopens the question of what a prediction is. There is also a
persistent, unresolved disagreement between measurements of the expansion
rate.

## Open questions

- Is dark matter a substance, or a sign that gravity is wrong on large
  scales? What observation would settle it?
- Is inflation a theory that could have been refuted, or a framework
  flexible enough to survive anything?
- If a multiverse is implied, what counts as a prediction — and does
  anthropic reasoning explain or merely excuse?
- Is the Hubble tension a measurement problem, or the first real crack in
  the concordance model?
- Cosmology has one sample. What does statistical inference mean at all
  with N = 1?
