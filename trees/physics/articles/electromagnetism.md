# Electromagnetism

[← Physics](../index.md) · `PH_EM`

The first field theory, and the one that forces every later revolution:
Maxwell's equations aren't invariant under Galilean transformations, which
is what makes relativity unavoidable rather than optional. The conceptual
jump is the field itself — energy and momentum located in apparently empty
space rather than in matter. Gauge freedom shows up here as a seemingly
harmless redundancy in the potentials, and later turns out to be the
organizing principle behind every fundamental interaction. The
Aharonov-Bohm effect complicates the easy dismissal of the potentials as
bookkeeping.

## Open questions

- Is the electromagnetic field a thing in the world, or a way of tracking
  how charges will affect each other?
- What is the status of the potentials, given Aharonov-Bohm — pure gauge,
  or physical after all?
- Why should a redundancy in our description turn out to be the deepest
  structural principle in fundamental physics?
- What does it mean that Maxwell's equations already contained relativity
  before anyone noticed it was there?
- Where does the classical theory actually break, and is each breakdown a
  hint about deeper structure or just a limit of applicability?
