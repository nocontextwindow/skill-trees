## Special Relativity (`PH_SR`)

Starting from two postulates — the laws of physics are the same in all
inertial frames, and the speed of light in vacuum is the same in all
inertial frames — Einstein derived that simultaneity, time intervals, and
lengths are all frame-dependent, unifying space and time into a single
Minkowski spacetime with an invariant interval. Mass-energy equivalence
(E = mc²) falls out as a consequence rather than being assumed. The
counterintuitive parts (time dilation, length contraction, the relativity
of simultaneity) are best absorbed through worked examples (the twin
paradox, muon decay) rather than the postulates alone.

## General Relativity (`PH_GR`)

Einstein's generalization of relativity to accelerated frames and gravity:
gravity isn't a force propagating through flat space but the curvature of
spacetime itself, sourced by mass-energy via the Einstein field equations.
Requires differential geometry (manifolds, metrics, curvature tensors) as
mathematical machinery, which is a real jump in prerequisite difficulty
from anything earlier in the tree. The payoff is some of the most
experimentally spectacular physics that exists — gravitational lensing,
frame dragging, gravitational waves (confirmed by LIGO in 2015), and black
holes, whose interior physics is exactly where GR and quantum mechanics
visibly fail to be reconciled.

**Resources:**
- Sean Carroll, *Spacetime and Geometry: An Introduction to General Relativity*
- James Hartle, *Gravity: An Introduction to Einstein's General Relativity* (gentler, physics-first approach)

## Quantum Mechanics (`PH_QM`)

The theory of the very small: physical quantities are represented by
operators on a Hilbert space, states evolve deterministically by the
Schrödinger equation between measurements, but measurement itself yields
probabilistic outcomes tied to the Born rule — the so-called measurement
problem, which is still unresolved and drives the interpretive debates
covered in `PH_PHILO`. Entanglement, the uncertainty principle, and the
double-slit experiment are the standard entry points; understanding why
Bell's theorem rules out local hidden-variable explanations is the point at
which QM stops being "weird" and starts being a precise, falsifiable claim
about the structure of reality.
