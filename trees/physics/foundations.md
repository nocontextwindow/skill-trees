## Math Prerequisites: Calculus / Linear Algebra / Diff Eq (`PH_MATH`)

Physics is written in the language of calculus (rates of change, integrals
as accumulated quantities), linear algebra (vector spaces, eigenvalues —
indispensable once you reach quantum mechanics, where observables are
literally linear operators), and ordinary/partial differential equations
(Newton's second law, the wave equation, the Schrödinger equation are all
ODEs or PDEs). Multivariable calculus and vector calculus (gradient,
divergence, curl) are the specific tools that unlock electromagnetism.
Skimping here is the single most common reason people bounce off physics
later — the physical ideas are usually simpler than the notation makes
them look.

- Mary Boas, *Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences*

## Classical Mechanics (`PH_CLASS`)

Newtonian mechanics (forces, momentum, energy conservation) is the entry
point, but the deeper payoff is the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian
reformulations: the principle of least action, generalized coordinates,
and Noether's theorem, which shows that every continuous symmetry of a
system corresponds to a conserved quantity (time-translation symmetry
gives energy conservation, spatial-translation symmetry gives momentum
conservation). This reformulation isn't just elegance for its own sake —
it's the direct conceptual ancestor of the formalism used in quantum
mechanics and quantum field theory later in the tree.

**Resources:**
- Kleppner & Kolenkow, *An Introduction to Mechanics*
- John Taylor, *Classical Mechanics*
