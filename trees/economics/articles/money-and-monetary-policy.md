# Money & Monetary Policy

[← Economics](../index.md) · `ECON_MONEY`

What money is, where it comes from, and what a central bank can and can't
do with it. The definitional question is not academic: a commodity story,
a credit story, and a state-theory story imply different limits on
policy. Post-2008 experience embarrassed several confident positions at
once — large balance sheet expansion without the predicted inflation,
then inflation arriving on a different mechanism — which is a useful
reminder of how weakly held the empirical ground is under strongly held
views.

## Open questions

- Is money best understood as a commodity that won a competition, a
  credit relation, or a creature of the state — and does the answer
  change what policy can do?
- Through which channel does monetary policy actually transmit, and how
  much of the effect is expectation management rather than the rate?
- Why did large-scale balance sheet expansion not produce the inflation
  quantity-theoretic reasoning predicted, and what does that concede?
- What does central bank independence protect against, and what does it
  insulate from accountability?
- Is there a stable price level worth targeting when relative prices are
  moving for real reasons?
- What determines whether a currency retains value — enforcement,
  expectations, tax demand, or the absence of an alternative?
