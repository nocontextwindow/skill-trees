# Macroeconomics

[← Economics](../index.md) · `ECON_MACRO`

The study of aggregates — output, employment, inflation, growth — in a
field that cannot run controlled experiments on its subject. That
constraint shapes everything: identification comes from assumptions the
model itself supplies, schools disagree for decades without resolution,
and relationships that look stable get refit each time they break. The
aggregation step is the philosophically loaded one, since a
representative agent may either be a harmless simplification or the exact
place where the interesting behavior (heterogeneity, coordination
failure, distribution) is destroyed.

## Open questions

- What is actually being aggregated? Is the representative agent a
  simplification or where the important dynamics get thrown away?
- Which macro relationships have held out of sample, and which keep
  getting respecified after they fail?
- How much of the disagreement between schools is empirical, and how much
  is prior commitment that no available data can settle?
- Are recessions coordination failures, slow relative-price adjustment,
  or real shocks — and can the right answer differ by episode?
- What would count as evidence against a macro model, given that its own
  assumptions supply the identification?
- Does the profession's forecasting record imply anything about the
  validity of its causal claims, or are those separable?
