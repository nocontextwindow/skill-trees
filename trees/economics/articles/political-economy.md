# Political Economy

[← Economics](../index.md) · `ECON_POLECON`

Where economic analysis stops assuming a benevolent planner and starts
modeling the state as an actor with its own interests. Public choice,
rent-seeking, and regulatory capture are the standard tools; the harder
material is the reverse direction, where economic arguments function less
as analysis than as justification for a coalition that already formed.
This is also the branch where economics runs into questions it cannot
settle internally — what property is, what distribution is owed — and has
to hand them to political philosophy or smuggle in an answer.

## Open questions

- Most policy economics assumes a planner who implements it — what
  changes when the planner has its own interests?
- Is regulatory capture a corruption of good design, or the predicted
  outcome of any design that concentrates benefits and diffuses costs?
- How much of observed policy is explained by ideas, and how much by
  whoever was organized enough to lobby for it?
- Do economic arguments move policy, or supply justification after the
  fact? What would distinguish the two?
- What does economics say about property and distribution that isn't
  downstream of a prior political commitment?
- If both markets and states fail predictably, on what basis is a
  particular mix chosen — evidence, or ideology with a model attached?
