# Development Economics

[← Economics](../index.md) · `ECON_DEV`

The question of why some places are rich and others are not, which is
either the most important question in economics or the one it has least
succeeded at. Explanations divide roughly into institutions, geography,
culture, and historical path dependence, and the arguments between them
have proven hard to settle because the units of observation are countries
and there are not many of them. The recent turn toward randomized field
experiments bought internal validity at some cost in scope: it can
establish what worked in a district and says much less about what makes a
country grow.

## Open questions

- Institutions, geography, culture, or history — is the disagreement
  about which cause is true, or about which is tractable?
- What can a field experiment in a set of villages establish that
  generalizes to a national policy?
- Does growth come from many small things done right, or from a few
  structural transformations that no policy can schedule?
- Why did convergence happen for some countries and not others, and does
  any theory identify which in advance?
- What is aid for, once humanitarian relief is separated from growth
  policy? Are the two ever in tension?
- Are the institutions credited with growth causes of it, or things a
  country can afford once it is already growing?
