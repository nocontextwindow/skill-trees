# Game Theory

[← Economics](../index.md) · `ECON_GAME`

The formal study of situations where the best thing to do depends on what
others do. Its real contribution is a vocabulary — commitment, credible
threat, signaling, common knowledge — that sharpens reasoning about
strategic situations even when the formalism isn't used. The trouble is
predictive: most interesting games have many equilibria, refinement
criteria for choosing among them are contested, and repeated-game results
show that under mild conditions nearly any outcome can be sustained. It
is often unclear whether a game-theoretic account is a prediction or a
retrospective story with equations.

## Open questions

- What licenses selecting one equilibrium when a game has many? Is
  equilibrium refinement a theory or a taste?
- How much is the assumption of common knowledge of rationality doing,
  and how gracefully do results degrade without it?
- If the folk theorem says almost anything can be an equilibrium of a
  repeated game, what prediction is left?
- When does modeling a situation as a game misdescribe it — because the
  players, payoffs, or rules were themselves chosen by someone?
- Does credible commitment work the same way when the device is
  reputation rather than an enforceable contract?
- Which real strategic insights come from the math, and which come from
  the vocabulary alone?
