# Free Will & Determinism

[← Personal Philosophy](../index.md) · `PP_FREEWILL`

If every event is fixed by prior causes, it's unclear what's left of
choice; if some events aren't, randomness doesn't obviously deliver
agency either. Compatibilism tries to dissolve the dilemma by redefining
freedom as acting on one's own reasons without external compulsion.
Everything in ethics that involves praise, blame, or responsibility is
downstream of how this resolves — which is why "it doesn't matter
practically" is a suspiciously convenient answer.

## Open questions

- Is volition a genuine causal power, or the experience of a process
  already determined?
- Does compatibilism resolve the problem or rename it?
- Can moral responsibility survive hard determinism — and if not, what
  replaces it?
- If choice is real, at what level does it operate: over actions, over
  attention, over what I let myself think about?
- Does my answer here actually match how I treat other people's failures?
