# Role, Duty & the Binding Figure

[← Personal Philosophy](../index.md) · `PP_ROLE`

Some positions in a group are structural: the person others calibrate
against, whose steadiness is what allows everyone else to be less steady.
The patriarch, the founder, the one who has to be present because his
absence is felt as a loss of orientation. The open questions are whether
that role is a natural feature of how groups organize or a contingent
cultural arrangement, what it costs the person occupying it, and whether
duties attach to a role independently of whether one consented to it.

## Open questions

- Is the binding figure a real structural necessity in groups, or a
  cultural artifact that other arrangements could replace?
- What does occupying that role actually cost, and is the cost payable
  indefinitely?
- Can role-duties bind someone who never consented to the role?
- How does an ethics centered on the individual accommodate obligations
  that come from position rather than choice?
- What distinguishes the binding figure from the merely controlling one?
