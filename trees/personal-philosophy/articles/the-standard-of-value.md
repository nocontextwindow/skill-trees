# The Standard of Value

[← Personal Philosophy](../index.md) · `PP_STANDARD`

Given that there are values, what's the measure — the thing against which
everything else counts as good or bad? Candidates: life or flourishing,
pleasure, preference satisfaction, duty, divine command, social utility.
The Aristotelian and neo-Aristotelian lines take life or flourishing as
the standard and try to derive an "ought" from facts about what kind of
entity we are; the standing objection is that this either smuggles in the
normativity it claims to derive, or delivers a standard too thin to settle
real cases.

## Open questions

- What is my standard, stated in one sentence, and does it actually decide
  hard cases or just sound good?
- Can an "ought" be derived from an "is"? If not, where does normativity
  enter?
- If life is the standard: whose life, over what time horizon, and how are
  conflicts between lives resolved?
- Is happiness the goal, the byproduct, or a bad target that corrupts when
  aimed at directly?
- Does my stated standard match the one revealed by my actual choices?
