# Gnosticism & Dualism

[← Esotericism & Religion](../index.md) · `ES_GNOSTIC`

A family of late-antique movements sharing one intuition: the material
world is defective, its maker is not the highest god, and salvation comes
through knowledge of one's true origin rather than through faith or works.
Scholars now seriously dispute whether "Gnosticism" names a real movement
at all or is a category assembled by its opponents for the convenience of
condemning it. The underlying problem outlives the label either way — if
the world is badly made, then either its god isn't good, isn't in charge,
or the world isn't what it appears to be, and every tradition has to pick
one. Dualist structures recur far beyond the ancient world, and they carry
a political shape with them: a worldview in which the visible order is
illegitimate by construction is a different thing to hold than one in which
it is merely imperfect.

## Open questions

- Is dualism a better answer to the problem of evil than monotheist
  theodicy, or does it just move the problem one step back?
- Does "Gnosticism" pick out anything real, or is it an artifact of
  heresy-hunting?
- What follows politically from believing the world's order is defective
  at its root rather than reformable?
- Does salvation-by-knowledge necessarily imply an elite? Is there a
  version that doesn't?
- Where do secularized versions of this structure show up now, and are
  they doing the same psychological work?
