# Mysticism vs Rationalism

[← Esotericism & Religion](../index.md) · `ES_MYSTIC`

Mystical traditions claim a mode of knowing that isn't inferential —
direct, non-conceptual, reported as more certain than ordinary knowledge
while being notoriously hard to state, transmit, or check. The rationalist
reply is that a claim you cannot articulate, test, or hand to someone else
isn't knowledge, whatever it felt like from inside. The genuinely hard
cases are the ones where the practices reliably produce effects — sustained
attention, equanimity, the dissolution of the self-other boundary — that
appear real even when the metaphysical interpretation attached to them
doesn't follow. It's worth noting that the phenomenology is far better
documented than the interpretations: the reports converge across traditions
much more than the doctrines built on top of them do.

## Open questions

- Can there be non-propositional knowledge, or is that a category error
  dressed up as humility?
- Does the cross-cultural similarity of mystical reports point to a common
  object, or just to a common nervous system?
- If a practice reliably changes a person for the better, does its
  metaphysics need to be true for that to count?
- Where is the line between an experience that should revise your beliefs
  and one that merely feels like it should?
- What would falsify a mystical claim — and if nothing would, what does
  that tell you about the claim?
