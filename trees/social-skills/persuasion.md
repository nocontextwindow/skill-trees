## Persuasion & Rhetoric (`SOC_PERSUADE`)

Aristotle's three modes (ethos, pathos, logos) as the baseline framework;
rhetorical devices like irony, analogy, and repetition as force multipliers
on top of a sound argument — not substitutes for one.

**Status:** [ ]

**Resources:**
- Aristotle, *Rhetoric*
- Robert Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*

## Influence (`SOC_INFLUENCE`)

Cialdini's six (now seven) principles — reciprocity, commitment/consistency,
social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity — as a checklist for both
using influence deliberately and noticing it being used on you.

**Status:** [ ]

**Resources:**
- Robert Cialdini, *Influence*

---

## Debate (`SOC_DEBATE`)

Structured argument under adversarial conditions — the actual skill is
staying inside the strongest version of your own argument while accurately
representing the opponent's, rather than winning on rhetorical tricks that
don't survive scrutiny.
