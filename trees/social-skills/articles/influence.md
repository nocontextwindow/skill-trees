# Influence

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_INFLUENCE`

The standing capacity to affect what other people do, as distinct from
winning any particular exchange. It's built out of track record,
accumulated reciprocity, being present where decisions actually get made,
and the perception that you're worth keeping on side. The classic
compliance principles — reciprocity, commitment, social proof, liking,
authority, scarcity — describe real effects, though several canonical
studies have had replication trouble and the effect sizes are considerably
smaller than the popular presentations suggest. The structural point matters
more than the tactics: influence behaves like a stock rather than a flow,
spending it on small things leaves nothing for the things that matter, and
most of it gets built in situations where you weren't trying to build
anything.

## Open questions

- Is influence a resource that depletes when used, or one that compounds
  through use?
- What builds influence that can't be done deliberately without spoiling
  the thing that made it work?
- How do you tell real influence from simply being liked?
- When should you spend influence on something you already know you'll
  lose?
- Does influence over people who can't tell they're being influenced count
  as the same thing?
