# Debate

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_DEBATE`

Structured disagreement conducted in front of an audience. Competitive
debate optimizes for winning before judges, which is not the same target as
being right — it rewards speed, framing, and exploitation of the format,
and trains people to argue either side with equal facility. That's a real
skill and a real hazard at once, since fluency in defending arbitrary
positions makes it easier to defend your own bad ones convincingly. The
transferable parts are narrower and more valuable: steelmanning before
replying, locating the actual crux instead of trading peripheral points,
and identifying whether a disagreement is empirical, definitional, or about
values — because only the first kind can be settled by evidence.

## Open questions

- Does practising both sides of arguments improve judgment or corrode it?
- How do you locate the crux when both parties have an interest in keeping
  it vague?
- What's the honest thing to do when you notice mid-argument that you're
  wrong but winning?
- Who is the real audience in most disagreements, and does that change what
  counts as a good argument?
- Is there a form of disagreement that leaves both people better off, and
  what does it require from both of them?
