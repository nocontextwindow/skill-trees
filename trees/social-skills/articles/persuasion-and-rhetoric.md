# Persuasion & Rhetoric

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_PERSUADE`

Moving someone from one position to another using words. The classical
division into argument, character, and emotion has held up better than most
two-thousand-year-old frameworks, and the modern research maps onto it
tolerably well — audiences process centrally or peripherally depending on
their motivation and ability to engage. What's clear from the evidence is
that arguments rarely move people directly: identity-protective reasoning
means a well-made counterargument frequently entrenches the position it
attacks, especially when the belief is tied to group membership. What works
better is a set of lower-status forms — asking questions, granting ground,
letting someone arrive at the conclusion themselves — which are also slower
and considerably less satisfying to use.

## Open questions

- If direct argument tends to entrench, when is arguing ever the right
  move?
- What separates persuasion from manipulation — consent, method, truth, or
  whether you'd describe what you're doing out loud?
- Is it possible to persuade someone without imposing a status cost on
  changing their mind?
- Should you be persuadable by the techniques you use? What does it mean
  about them if you're not?
- How much of persuasion is technique, and how much is simply being someone
  whose judgment is already trusted?
