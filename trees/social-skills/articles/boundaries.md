# Boundaries

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_BOUND`

The conversational side of limits: saying no, declining requests, ending
things, and stating terms without it becoming a confrontation. Much of the
skill is in delivery — a limit stated once, warmly, without a pile of
justification is easier to accept than one defended at length, because
over-explaining invites the other person to negotiate the reasons instead
of accepting the limit. The genuinely hard cases are asymmetric ones, where
the other party can impose real costs for the no. It's worth separating
two problems people routinely conflate: the internal one of working out
what your limits actually are, and the external one of communicating them.
Most effort goes into the second while the first is still unresolved.

## Open questions

- How much explanation does a no need, and what does additional explanation
  actually buy?
- Where is the line between a boundary and a punishment?
- What do you do when the cost of holding a limit clearly exceeds the value
  of the limit?
- Is it possible to say no *well* to someone who simply won't accept it, or
  is that outside your control entirely?
- How do you find out what your limits are before someone tests them?
