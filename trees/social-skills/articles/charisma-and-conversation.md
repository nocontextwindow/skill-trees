# Conversational Skill & Charisma

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_CONVO`

Charisma looks like a trait and behaves partly like a skill. Its observable
components — expressive nonverbals, signals of warmth, responsiveness,
giving the other person status — are trainable, and rated charisma does
respond to training in experimental work, though the effects are modest and
the construct is measured almost entirely by how other people rate you,
which makes the whole thing somewhat circular. Conversation itself has
identifiable structure: turn-taking, question-asking (people who ask
follow-up questions are reliably better liked), and calibrated disclosure.
The tension worth sitting with is that the components can be practised, and
practising them badly reads as insincere — which is worse than not doing
them at all.

## Open questions

- Is charisma a skill, a trait, or a reputation? Does the answer change
  what you would practise?
- Why does deliberate warmth read as fake at low skill and genuine at high
  skill — is anything different besides execution?
- How much of being good at conversation is just real curiosity, and can
  curiosity be cultivated on purpose?
- What separates someone people enjoy from someone people trust, and can
  one person be both?
- Does charisma work on the people you'd actually want it to work on?
