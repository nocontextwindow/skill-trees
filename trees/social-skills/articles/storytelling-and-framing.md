# Storytelling & Narrative Framing

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_STORY`

Structured narrative is how information gets retained and how it acquires
stakes. The reasonably supported findings: narratives are recalled better
than lists, and an absorbed audience counter-argues less than one being
presented with an explicit case — the transportation effect. That second
finding is exactly why storytelling is a persuasion technique and why it's
ethically loaded rather than neutral. Most of the work is done by
structure: a specific person, a want, an obstacle, a change. The
characteristic failure of analytically-minded people is to deliver the
conclusion instead of the sequence, which is efficient, correct, and
completely forgettable.

## Open questions

- Is narrative persuasive because it's clearer, or because it lowers
  scrutiny?
- What gets distorted when a real situation is shaped to fit story
  structure — and is the distortion ever worth it?
- Can you tell true stories about yourself repeatedly without the retelling
  quietly replacing the memory?
- When does an audience deserve the argument instead of the story?
- Why does the same story land in one room and die in another?
