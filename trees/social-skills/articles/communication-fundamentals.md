# Communication Fundamentals

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_COMM`

Getting something from one head into another with the meaning intact. The
main obstacle isn't vocabulary but the curse of knowledge — the systematic
inability to model what a listener doesn't already know, which persists
even when you're explicitly warned about it. A second complication is that
almost every message carries a relational claim alongside its content (who
we are to each other, who is entitled to say this), and people respond to
the relational layer first. Listening is the underweighted half:
comprehension checks and reflecting back what you heard do more for
accuracy than any improvement in how clearly you express yourself. A large
share of what gets called poor communication turns out to be unstated
disagreement about what the conversation is for.

## Open questions

- How do you model what someone doesn't know, when your own knowledge is
  invisible to you from the inside?
- What relational message are you sending by default, and can you actually
  choose it?
- When someone objects, how do you tell a disagreement about content from
  one about standing?
- Is being understood always the goal — when is precision the wrong move?
- What here is genuinely improvable by study, versus fluency that only
  comes from volume?
