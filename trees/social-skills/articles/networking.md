# Networking

[← Social Skills](../index.md) · `SOC_NETWORK`

Deliberately building and holding a set of relationships that carry
information and opportunity. The best-supported finding is the strength of
weak ties: most opportunities arrive through acquaintances rather than
close contacts, because distant contacts know things your immediate circle
doesn't. That implies the useful structure is breadth plus a few bridges
into unrelated clusters — close to the opposite of what forms naturally,
since people drift toward denser contact with people already like them. The
unresolved tension is instrumentality: a relationship maintained for what
it produces tends to produce less, people are good at detecting the
difference, and the resolution most experienced people converge on — be
genuinely useful and don't keep score — is either moral wisdom or a more
sophisticated strategy, and it isn't obvious which.

## Open questions

- Can relationships be maintained instrumentally without becoming worth
  less?
- Is "be genuinely useful and don't keep score" a moral position, or just
  the optimal long-run strategy wearing one?
- What is a weak tie actually worth, and how many can one person hold
  before they stop being ties?
- Does network advantage compound because of position, or because position
  selects for people who were going to do well anyway?
- What do you owe someone whose network you benefited from?
