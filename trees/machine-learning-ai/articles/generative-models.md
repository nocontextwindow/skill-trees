# Generative Models

[← Machine Learning / AI](../index.md) · `ML_GEN`

Models of the data distribution itself rather than a decision boundary
through it — adversarial, diffusion, and autoregressive approaches among
others. Evaluation is the standing methodological problem, since the
trainable objective (likelihood, or a discriminator's judgment) and the
thing anyone cares about (sample quality, usefulness, novelty) come apart
routinely. Trained on web-scale corpora, these models also make the
line between generalization and memorization genuinely hard to draw,
which is a technical question with immediate legal and ethical weight.

## Open questions

- How do you evaluate a generative model when the objective and the
  property you care about disagree?
- When is an output novel and when is it retrieval? What test
  distinguishes them for a model trained on most of the internet?
- What does it mean to have learned a distribution you can only sample
  from and never inspect?
- Why does the diffusion formulation work as well as it does — is the
  connection to nonequilibrium physics load-bearing or a convenient
  derivation?
- What obligations does the training-data-to-output relationship create,
  and why does the technical framing make them so easy to not see?
- Does conditioning give real control over the output, or the appearance
  of control within the region the model already covers well?
