# AI Alignment & Safety

[← Machine Learning / AI](../index.md) · `ML_ALIGN`

Getting systems to do what was intended, and knowing when they haven't.
It splits into problems with different epistemic status: specification
and robustness failures are observable today and studied empirically,
while arguments about strongly capable future systems are mostly
conceptual and hard to test in advance. Treating those two as one
subject, in either direction, is a common error. The deepest version of
the problem is that training against human evaluation optimizes for
looking good to an evaluator, and those two targets separate exactly
where the stakes are highest.

## Open questions

- Which claims here are empirical and which are arguments not yet
  testable — and how should my confidence differ between them?
- Is specification failure a solvable engineering problem, or a
  restatement of the fact that human values aren't written down anywhere?
- Does training a system to be rated well by humans select for being good
  or for being convincing? How would you tell them apart?
- What does oversight mean for a system whose outputs a human can no
  longer check?
- Do near-term harms and long-horizon risk arguments actually compete for
  the same attention, or is that framing itself doing work for someone?
- Who is the "we" in "we should align AI to human values", and what
  happens to the problem when that group disagrees?
