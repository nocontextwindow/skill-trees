# Descriptive Statistics

[← Statistics](../index.md) · `STAT_DESC`

Summarizing data before inferring anything from it — and the stage where
the most consequential decisions get made with the least scrutiny. Every
summary is a lossy compression, and the choice of which statistic to report
is already an argument: a mean and a median tell different stories about
the same skewed distribution, and both are "correct." Aggregation is where
Simpson's paradox lives, where outlier handling becomes an ethical rather
than technical question, and where a chart can be honest in every number
and still mislead in its overall impression. Exploratory data analysis is
also in tension with confirmatory inference — the more you look before you
test, the less your test means.

## Open questions

- When is a summary statistic a legitimate compression and when is it
  destroying the thing I actually needed to see?
- How do I explore a dataset thoroughly without burning the ability to make
  an honest inferential claim about it afterward?
- What is my actual rule for handling outliers, stated in advance, and can
  I defend it to someone who wants the opposite conclusion?
- At what point does a disaggregation stop revealing structure and start
  manufacturing it?
- Which visual encodings do I trust to convey uncertainty, and which ones
  do I now know produce false confidence in readers?
- Is "let the data speak for itself" ever anything other than a hidden set
  of choices?
