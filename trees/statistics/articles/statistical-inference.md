# Statistical Inference

[← Statistics](../index.md) · `STAT_INFER`

Going from a sample to a claim about a population, under the frequentist
framework of sampling distributions, p-values, and confidence intervals.
This is the most widely taught and most widely misused part of statistics.
Nearly everyone who reports a p-value can state its definition and still
reasons about it as if it were the probability the hypothesis is false; a
95% confidence interval is a statement about the procedure, not about the
particular interval in front of you. The replication crisis made it clear
that the machinery is not self-protecting: p-hacking, garden-of-forking-
paths analysis, and publication bias can produce a literature of
significant results with almost no true effects in it. Meanwhile the
Neyman-Pearson error-control framing and the Fisherian evidential framing
are genuinely different philosophies that got fused into one classroom
procedure.

## Open questions

- What question does a p-value actually answer, and is it ever the question
  I care about?
- Is null hypothesis significance testing salvageable with better
  discipline, or structurally the wrong tool for most of what it's used for?
- How much of the replication crisis is bad statistics versus bad
  incentives — and would fixing only the statistics change anything?
- What does statistical significance buy me that a well-reported effect
  size and interval do not?
- When is a pre-registered, underpowered study worse than no study at all?
- How should I read a literature I know is filtered by publication bias
  without either accepting it or dismissing it wholesale?
