# Morality & Social Enforcement

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_MORALENF`

Whether society may enforce a moral code beyond preventing force and
fraud. One side holds that a shared moral code is itself part of what
holds a society together, so its erosion is a genuine social harm; the
other that enforcing morality on the unwilling is exactly the tyranny
liberty exists to prevent. Both positions have to say something about
informal enforcement — shame, exclusion, reputation — which operates
whether or not law does.

## Open questions

- May society enforce morality beyond preventing harm? Where exactly is
  the line, and am I consistent about it?
- Is informal enforcement — shame, ostracism — categorically different
  from legal coercion, or just cheaper?
- Is a shared moral code a real precondition of social stability, or a
  claim made by whoever holds the current code?
- Who decides which morality gets enforced when a society is genuinely
  plural?
- What's the strongest case for a purely liberal, non-enforcing position?
