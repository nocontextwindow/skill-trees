# Tradition & Inheritance

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_TRADITION`

Whether inherited institutions carry accumulated knowledge that no
individual reasoner could reconstruct, or whether they mostly encode the
interests of whoever won earlier disputes. A related and sharper question
is how much of a given political settlement is downstream of a particular
religious inheritance — and if the answer is "a great deal," whether the
settlement survives once that inheritance recedes.

## Open questions

- How much of the individual-rights tradition is downstream of a specific
  religious inheritance, and what follows if it's most of it?
- Does a liberal order run on borrowed moral capital, and what happens
  when it's spent?
- When is tradition accumulated wisdom and when is it a frozen accident?
- Can a tradition be deliberately maintained by people who don't believe
  its underlying claims?
- What's the strongest case that inherited institutions deserve no
  presumption at all?
