# Tradition & Inheritance

`POL_TRADITION` · [← Political Philosophy](../index.md)

Whether inherited institutions carry knowledge no individual could reconstruct, or just encode who won earlier.

## Questions

- How much of the individual-rights tradition is downstream of a specific religious inheritance, and what follows if it's most of it?
- Does a liberal order run on borrowed moral capital, and what happens when it's spent?
- When is tradition accumulated wisdom and when is it a frozen accident?
- Can a tradition be maintained by people who don't believe its underlying claims?
- What's the strongest case that inherited institutions deserve no presumption at all?

## Challenges & perspectives

- Burke: society is a partnership across generations; reformers underestimate what they're dismantling.
- Chesterton's fence: don't remove what you can't explain the purpose of.
- Hayek on tradition as distributed knowledge no planner has access to.
- Counter: "accumulated wisdom" is unfalsifiable, and plenty of traditions were simply enforced.
- Claim that Western individualism has specifically Christian theological roots — with the question of whether it survives their recession.
