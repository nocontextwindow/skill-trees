# Human Nature & What Politics Must Assume

`POL_NATURE` · [← Political Philosophy](../index.md)

Every political philosophy encodes a theory of what people are like; most disagreements are downstream of that.

## Questions

- What does my politics assume about human nature, and would I defend that assumption on its own?
- Is human nature fixed enough to build institutions on, or plastic enough that institutions shape it?
- If people are worse than a system assumes, does it fail gracefully or catastrophically?
- Which failures of an order are design failures versus failures of the people in it?
- Should institutions be designed for people as they are or as they could become?

## Challenges & perspectives

- Hobbes: bad enough that almost any sovereign beats the alternative.
- Rousseau: decent by nature, corrupted by society.
- Blank slate vs. evolved-nature accounts, with very different policy implications.
- "Constrained vs. unconstrained vision" as the underlying split between right and left.
- Utopian projects that assumed plasticity have a poor track record — though so has assuming fixity.
