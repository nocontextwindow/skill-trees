# Human Nature & What Politics Must Assume

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_NATURE`

Every political philosophy encodes a theory of what people are like — how
self-interested, how tractable, how capable of governing themselves — and
most of the disagreement between traditions is downstream of that
encoding rather than of the explicit arguments. Pessimistic accounts
justify strong authority; optimistic ones justify wide latitude; accounts
that treat human nature as plastic justify projects aimed at changing it.

## Open questions

- What does my politics assume about human nature, and would I defend that
  assumption on its own?
- Is human nature fixed enough to build institutions on, or plastic enough
  that institutions shape it?
- If people are worse than a system assumes, does the system fail
  gracefully or catastrophically?
- Which failures of a political order are failures of design versus
  failures of the people in it?
- Should institutions be designed for people as they are or as they could
  become?
