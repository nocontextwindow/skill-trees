# Family, Sex & Social Reproduction

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_FAMILY`

How a society produces its next generation and transmits itself is a
political question, not merely a private one — which is why family
structure, sexual norms, and the relations between the sexes are perennial
sites of political conflict. The disputes here tend to conflate three
separable things: empirical claims about what actually follows from a
given arrangement, normative claims about what should be valued, and
strategic claims about whether a proposed remedy would work.

## Open questions

- What's the strongest form of the feminist case, and precisely where does
  my disagreement begin — on facts, on values, or on strategy?
- Is there a secular case for restrictive sexual norms that stands without
  religious premises, and does it hold?
- What's actually wrong with hedonism as a life-organizing principle, in
  argument rather than in distaste?
- Is the family a pre-political institution the state should defer to, or
  one arrangement among several?
- How much of what looks like nature here is convention, and how would I
  tell?
