# Ideology, Persuasion & Manufactured Belief

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_IDEOLOGY`

How political belief is actually produced at scale: framing, repetition,
control over which questions are askable, and the management of what
counts as respectable opinion. The theoretical difficulty is finding a
principled distinction between legitimate persuasion and manipulation that
doesn't reduce to "framing I agree with is persuasion, framing I don't is
propaganda."

## Open questions

- Is there a principled line between persuasion and manipulation —
  consent, transparency of method, reversibility — or only a matter of
  degree?
- Who controls which questions are askable, and by what mechanism rather
  than conspiracy?
- Can a population be said to hold a belief freely if the alternatives
  were never presented?
- How much of my own political belief would survive discovering how it was
  formed?
- Is ideology an error to be corrected or an unavoidable feature of
  political cognition?
