# Revolution & Political Change

`POL_CHANGE` · [← Political Philosophy](../index.md)

When existing order may be overthrown rather than reformed, and what predictably follows.

## Questions

- When is revolution justified — stated in advance rather than in retrospect?
- Why do revolutions so often produce something worse before something better?
- Is gradual reform a principled position or a preference of the already comfortable?
- What distinguishes a founding from a coup, other than outcome?
- What would have to be true of an order for me to judge it beyond reform?

## Challenges & perspectives

- Locke's right of revolution when government breaks its trust.
- Burke's counter: the inherited order embeds more than critics can see, so replacement costs more than predicted.
- Thermidor pattern: radical phase, terror, reaction, strongman.
- Justified ex post by what preceded, judged by what followed — which makes the ex ante question genuinely hard.
- Nonviolent-resistance research suggesting it outperforms armed revolt on both success and aftermath.
