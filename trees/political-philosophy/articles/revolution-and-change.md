# Revolution & Political Change

[← Political Philosophy](../index.md) · `POL_CHANGE`

When existing order may be overthrown rather than reformed, and what
predictably happens when it is. Revolutions tend to be justified by the
injustice of what preceded them and judged by what followed, which makes
the ex ante question — when is it *permissible*, not when did it *work* —
genuinely hard. Gradualist positions hold that inherited order embeds more
than its critics can see, so its replacement reliably costs more than
predicted.

## Open questions

- When, if ever, is revolution justified — and can that be stated in
  advance rather than in retrospect?
- Why do revolutions so often produce something worse before something
  better?
- Is gradual reform a principled position or a preference of those already
  comfortable?
- What distinguishes a founding from a coup, other than outcome?
- What would have to be true of an order for me to judge it beyond reform?
