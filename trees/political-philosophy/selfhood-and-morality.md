### Can a political order be built on rational self-interest, or does it collapse once scaled from personal ethics to policy? `POL_SELFISH`

Steelman first: the standard collectivist objection is that "virtue of
selfishness" as *personal* ethics is one thing, but as a *political*
foundation it either (a) has nothing to say about those who can't compete,
or (b) becomes a justification for exploitation dressed up as principle.
Rand's response is that rational egoism explicitly forbids the initiation
of force or fraud, so it isn't "might makes right" — it's closer to
"trade, not sacrifice, as the model of human relations." Does that
response actually hold?

### What's actually wrong with hedonism, beyond personal distaste? `POL_HEDON`

Rand's critique: hedonism mistakes the *result* of a life well-lived
(pleasure/happiness) for its *cause*, and treats feeling as a guide to
action instead of reason. Aristotle's eudaimonia is the classical
alternative — flourishing as the goal, pleasure as its byproduct, not its
target. Is there a real argument here, or is this just an aesthetic
preference for a certain kind of person?
