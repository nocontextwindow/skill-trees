# Visualization Techniques

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_VISUAL`

Deliberate mental imagery — either rehearsing the execution of an action or
picturing an outcome. The evidence splits sharply between the two. Motor
imagery, mentally rehearsing a specific physical or procedural skill, has
decent support in sports and skill acquisition, with measurable performance
effects and substantial overlap in neural activation with actual execution.
Outcome visualization — picturing yourself having already succeeded — looks
worse: several lines of work suggest it can *reduce* effort and preparation
by delivering some of the satisfaction in advance, and mental contrasting
(imagining the outcome, then the obstacles) is the better-supported
variant. Imagery vividness also varies enormously between people, up to
aphantasia, and what these techniques mean for someone at the low end is
not well addressed anywhere.

## Open questions

- Is the process/outcome distinction as clean as the research makes it
  sound, or does it break down in real cases?
- If a technique depends on vivid imagery, what is the substitute for
  people who don't have it?
- When does visualizing success substitute for pursuing it, and when does
  it prime it?
- How much of the sports-psychology result is imagery, and how much is just
  structured attention to the task?
- Is there a version of this that turns out to be planning with better
  marketing?
