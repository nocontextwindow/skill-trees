# Cognitive Control & Meta-Cognition

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_COGCTRL`

Noticing and intervening on your own thinking — where attention goes, what
gets ruminated on, how events get interpreted, how fast you react. This is
the most empirically solid part of the tree: cognitive reappraisal
reliably outperforms suppression in emotion-regulation research, attention
has known and unforgiving limits, and metacognitive accuracy — knowing what
you actually know — is poor and only modestly improvable. The transfer
claims for brain training mostly failed; people get better at the trained
task, not at cognition. The structural limit is recursive: the faculty
doing the monitoring is the same one being monitored, so the failures you
are worst at catching are precisely the ones you are worst at catching.

## Open questions

- How do you catch a distortion using the same apparatus that is producing
  it?
- Reappraisal versus acceptance — when is reframing a thought better than
  letting it stand?
- Is there a cost to constant self-monitoring, and what does it do to
  spontaneity over time?
- How much of "controlling your mind" is control, and how much is just
  choosing your inputs and environment?
- What is the honest evidence that any of this is trainable, rather than a
  stable difference between people?
