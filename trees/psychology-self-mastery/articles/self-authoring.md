# Self-Authoring & Personal Narrative

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_AUTHOR`

Treating your life as a story you are writing rather than one that is
happening to you. Narrative identity research finds that life stories have
measurable structure — redemption versus contamination sequences, degree of
agency, coherence — and that this structure correlates with wellbeing,
though the causal direction is genuinely unclear and could plausibly run
either way. Structured writing interventions have real but modest effects,
and the published effect sizes have shrunk as replications accumulated. The
deeper problem is honesty: a narrative is a selection from what happened,
and the same selection pressure that makes a story motivating is what makes
it inaccurate.

## Open questions

- Where is the line between constructing a narrative and lying to yourself
  about your own past?
- Does the correlation between redemptive stories and wellbeing mean the
  story is causing anything?
- Can you hold a story as deliberately *chosen* and still be moved by it?
- What do you owe the parts of the past that don't fit the story you've
  settled on?
- Is a life better lived as a narrative at all, or is that a literary form
  imposed on something that isn't shaped like one?
