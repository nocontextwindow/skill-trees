# Productivity Systems

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_PRODUCTIVITY`

Externalized methods for managing commitments and attention. Almost none of
the named systems have been tested — they are craft knowledge, plausible
and largely unvalidated, and their popularity tracks how good their authors
were at writing rather than how well they work. What *is* supported is
narrower: writing things down offloads memory, task-switching carries real
costs, and deciding in advance when and where you'll do something beats
intending to do it. The characteristic failure is that the system becomes
the work — maintaining it feels productive and defers the thing itself.
Worse, output is not value: a system optimized for throughput can make
someone extremely efficient at the wrong things for years.

## Open questions

- What's the minimum system that actually holds, and does anything beyond
  that cost more than it returns?
- How do you tell system maintenance from procrastination that has learned
  to look organized?
- Should a system optimize for volume of output, or for protecting the few
  things that actually matter?
- Does any of this survive contact with a genuinely unpredictable schedule,
  or is it all built for people who control their calendars?
- If you keep abandoning systems, is the answer a better system or a
  different question?
