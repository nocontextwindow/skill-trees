# Self-Hypnosis

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_HYPNO`

Inducing in yourself a state of focused absorption and reduced critical
evaluation, then delivering suggestions into it. Something real is going
on: suggestibility is a measurable, stable, strongly varying individual
trait, and hypnotic analgesia has reasonable clinical support. What is much
weaker is the folk apparatus built around it — "reprogramming the
subconscious," fixed induction scripts, claimed durable effects on
motivation, personality, or belief. There isn't even a settled account of
what hypnosis *is*: whether it's a distinct altered state or an
expectancy-and-role phenomenon remains genuinely disputed among people who
study it. Self-hypnosis specifically has thinner evidence than the
practitioner-led kind, and the mechanism by which one part of a mind
suspends another part's judgment is not obvious on any account.

## Open questions

- Is there a distinct hypnotic state, or is it expectancy plus absorbed
  role-play — and does the answer change how you'd use it?
- What does it mean to suggest something to yourself? Who is suggesting to
  whom?
- If suggestibility is trait-like, is this a technique available only to a
  subset of people?
- Is deliberately lowering your own critical evaluation ever a good idea
  for someone whose main project is thinking clearly?
- Where is the boundary between this, meditation, and focused self-talk —
  or is it one thing under three names?
