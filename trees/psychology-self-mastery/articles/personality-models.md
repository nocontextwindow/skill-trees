# Personality Models

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_PERSONALITY`

Competing schemes for describing stable individual differences. The Big
Five (and its HEXACO variant) is the empirically strongest: derived from
factor analysis of trait language, moderately heritable, fairly stable
across adulthood, and modestly predictive of real outcomes. Type systems —
Jungian typology, the instruments descended from it, the enneagram — are
far more popular and much weaker: poor test-retest reliability,
dichotomies imposed on continuous distributions, and predictive validity
that thins out under scrutiny. The interesting fact is that the weaker
systems are more *useful* to most people, probably because a type generates
a narrative and a percentile on a factor doesn't. Worth holding onto:
description is not mechanism, and knowing where you sit on a dimension
tells you almost nothing about why or what to do about it.

## Open questions

- Traits are stable-ish and heritable-ish — how much of what feels like
  choice is actually that?
- Do type systems survive as anything more than a shared vocabulary for
  talking about people, and is that worth having on its own?
- If a model is invalid but useful for self-reflection, what is the actual
  cost of using it?
- Does knowing your profile change what you should attempt, or only how you
  should attempt it?
- What is the real difference between a trait and a habit that has lasted
  long enough to look like one?
