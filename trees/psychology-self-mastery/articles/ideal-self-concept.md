# Ideal Self Concept

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_IDEAL`

Specifying on purpose who you are trying to become, instead of drifting
into it. The structure is old — a form the particular imperfectly
instantiates — and the modern research on self-discrepancy suggests the gap
between actual and ideal self is motivating under some conditions and
demoralizing under others, with the distinction between an ideal (what you
aspire to) and an ought (what you feel obligated to) appearing to matter.
The hardest part is authorship: an ideal assembled from what happens to be
admired in your immediate environment may just be an internalized status
ranking wearing philosophical clothes. And there is a real bind in the
specificity — an ideal precise enough to guide daily action is precise
enough to be wrong about what you should have become.

## Open questions

- How do you distinguish an ideal you actually endorse from one you
  absorbed from whoever was nearby?
- Should the ideal be reachable? What does an unreachable one do to someone
  over twenty years?
- When is the gap between actual and ideal fuel, and when is it just a
  standing indictment?
- How often should the ideal be revised, and how do you revise it without
  it being surrender?
- Does it need to be a single coherent figure, or can it be a set of
  standards with no person behind them?
