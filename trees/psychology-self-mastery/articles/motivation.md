# Motivation — Theory & Practice

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_MOTIVE`

Why anyone does anything. Self-determination theory — autonomy, competence,
relatedness, and the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction — is the dominant
framework and has reasonable support; expectancy-value and goal-setting
work adds that motivation tracks perceived probability of success times
value, discounted heavily by delay. This is also a field where replication
has been rough: ego depletion, the most-cited model of willpower as a
depletable resource, largely failed large preregistered replication, so
advice built on it should be held loosely. Most of the durable practical
advice reduces to one claim — that motivation is an unreliable input and
structure outperforms willpower — which raises the real question of whether
to cultivate motivation at all or design around needing it.

## Open questions

- Is motivation something to be cultivated, or an unreliable input to be
  engineered around?
- Does making an activity instrumental reliably kill intrinsic interest in
  it, or only under specific conditions?
- What actually happens to a person who does hard things without wanting
  to, repeatedly, for years?
- Can you want something on purpose — deliberately change what you value,
  not just what you do?
- Which of the things you call yourself unmotivated for do you simply not
  want?
