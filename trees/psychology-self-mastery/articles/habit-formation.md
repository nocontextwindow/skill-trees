# Habit Formation

[← Psychology & Self-Mastery](../index.md) · `PSY_HABIT`

Behaviour that runs on context cues rather than deliberation. The
best-supported mechanism is that repetition in a stable context gradually
shifts control from goal-directed to automatic, and implementation
intentions — committing in advance to a specific if-X-then-Y — have one of
the more robust effect sizes in the area. The popular fixed timelines for
"forming a habit" come from a small study with an enormous range, and
should not be treated as a number. What's underrated is that a habit is a
way of spending less attention, which makes the real design question not
*how* to automate but *what* to automate: automaticity buys you consistency
at the price of no longer noticing when the behaviour stopped making sense.

## Open questions

- Which decisions are worth automating, and which lose something important
  when they stop being chosen?
- Does character reduce to habit, or is there something left over once you
  subtract it?
- How do you build in a review that catches a habit which has outlived its
  purpose?
- Is identity-based habit change ("become the kind of person who…") doing
  real work, or restating the goal in a more flattering way?
- Why is breaking an entrenched habit so much harder than building one, and
  what actually works?
